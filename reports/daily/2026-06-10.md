### Idea 1 — Inventory Valuation Methods
**FR:** Évaluation des Stocks (CMUP / FIFO)

**Target learner:** Licence 2

**Pedagogical objective:** Students confuse stock flow assumptions with physical goods flow and cannot trace how the choice of CMUP (SYSCOHADA-recommended) versus FIFO produces different COGS figures and therefore different gross margins. They treat the two methods as interchangeable.

**Core interaction:** The learner enters a sequence of 3–6 purchase events and 2–4 issue events (date, quantity, unit cost). Two side-by-side stock cards update in real time — one for FIFO, one for CMUP — as entries are added. Toggling between methods animates the row-by-row recalculation. A summary strip shows the resulting COGS delta and its knock-on effect on gross profit.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: opening balance, purchase events (date, qty, unit cost), issue events (date, qty)
- Outputs: per-method stock card, closing stock value, COGS, gross profit comparison

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** CVP shows P&L sensitivity to cost changes; this playground shows *where* those costs originate. Amortization covers Class 2 asset write-down; Inventory covers Class 3 current-asset valuation — a distinct SYSCOHADA account class with no overlap.

---

### Idea 2 — Cash Flow Statement Builder (SYSCOHADA Format)
**FR:** Tableau des Flux de Trésorerie (TFT SYSCOHADA)

**Target learner:** Licence 3

**Pedagogical objective:** SYSCOHADA's Tableau des Flux de Trésorerie mandates the direct presentation method in three sections (operating / investing / financing), which differs from IFRS IAS 7's indirect operating section. Students routinely misclassify items — treating dividends received as financing, or loan repayments as operating — and cannot reconcile the ending cash figure back to the balance sheet.

**Core interaction:** Given a pre-built simplified balance sheet and income statement, the student drags 10–15 labelled transaction cards into the three TFT sections. The running total per section updates live; a reconciliation row compares the computed net cash change to the balance-sheet cash delta. Incorrect placements highlight with the relevant SYSCOHADA rule citation.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: editable balance sheet + income statement values; drag-and-drop transaction classification
- Outputs: completed TFT in SYSCOHADA format, reconciliation check (pass/fail with diff highlighted)

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Bank Reconciliation arrives at a single correct cash balance; TFT uses that balance as its starting point and explains where the cash came from across three activities. Direct pedagogical chain, zero overlap.

---

### Idea 3 — Financial Ratios Dashboard
**FR:** Tableau de Bord des Ratios Financiers

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master / practising professional

**Pedagogical objective:** Students calculate ratios in isolation without understanding which ratios signal related problems. They cannot connect a weak current ratio to a slow inventory turnover without seeing both move simultaneously, and they do not know SYSCOHADA-informed sector benchmarks for OHADA-zone companies.

**Core interaction:** The learner adjusts a simplified balance sheet and income statement via sliders (revenue, inventory days, debt level, equity). Twelve key ratios (4 categories: liquidity, solvency, profitability, activity) update in real time. A traffic-light matrix flags which ratios cross configurable sector benchmarks. An "explain" popover shows the formula and the SYSCOHADA account codes that feed it.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: balance sheet line items, income statement line items (sliders + number fields)
- Outputs: 12 ratios, benchmark comparison matrix (traffic-light), formula popover with SYSCOHADA account codes

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** None of the existing playgrounds work at the level of aggregated financial statements. TVM covers asset valuation in isolation; CVP covers the income statement; this playground synthesises across both the P&L and the balance sheet — the natural capstone for Licence 3.

### Idea 4 — Payroll Calculator (OHADA Zone)
**FR:** Calculateur de Fiche de Paie (Zone OHADA / CEMAC)

**Target learner:** Master / practising professional

**Pedagogical objective:** Students believe "gross pay minus tax = net pay." Real OHADA-zone payroll has 5–8 mandatory deduction layers (CNPS employer/employee shares, professional income tax, family allowances) governed by country-specific labour codes. Practitioners in the CEMAC zone routinely miscalculate employer total cost.

**Core interaction:** The learner enters gross salary, contract type (CDI/CDD), number of dependants, and selects a country (initially Cameroon / CEMAC). The playground generates a full itemised pay slip with each deduction line referencing its legal rate and SYSCOHADA account code (Class 4 payable, Class 6 charges). A what-if toggle shows the employer total-cost impact of a salary change.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: gross salary, country/zone selector, dependants count, contract type
- Outputs: itemised pay slip (employer + employee views), employer total cost, net-to-gross ratio, relevant legal-rate citations

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Personnel costs (Class 6 and Class 4 in SYSCOHADA) are untouched by every existing playground. Payroll directly exercises the `AccountingStandard` toggle already present in the shell and is uniquely relevant to the B2B audience (HR managers, SME owners).

### Idea 5 — Provisions & Contingent Liabilities
**FR:** Provisions pour Risques et Charges

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master

**Pedagogical objective:** Provisioning is consistently the most failed topic in SYSCOHADA Advanced Accounting exams. Students cannot distinguish a provision for depreciation (deterministic, already covered by the Depreciation playground) from a provision for risk (probabilistic, Class 1 liability) and cannot compute correct reversals when the liability partially crystallises.

**Core interaction:** The learner works through a three-period scenario: Year 1 — record a provision for a pending lawsuit using a probability-weighted estimate; Year 2 — update the estimate and compute the additional charge or partial reversal; Year 3 — the lawsuit settles (outcome revealed at runtime: full, partial, or excess). Journal entries auto-populate in a trial balance and the learner validates each posting. A waterfall chart shows the provision account balance evolving across all three periods.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: initial provision amount, probability estimate, Year 2 revised estimate, Year 3 settlement amount (randomly drawn from a range on scenario start)
- Outputs: 3-year journal entry sequence (student validates), per-year P&L impact, provision account waterfall chart

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry teaches debit/credit mechanics; this playground teaches *when* to provision and *how* to reverse — the reasoning layer above mechanics. Depreciation covers Class 2 asset provisions; this covers Class 1 liability provisions. No overlap in account class, logic, or interaction pattern.
