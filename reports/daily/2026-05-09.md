**Files (28 occurrences across 8 components):**

| File | Lines |
|------|-------|

| `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/TransactionJournal.svelte` | 382–383 |
| `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/BankStatementPanel.svelte` | 175 |
| `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/ScenarioWalkthrough.svelte` | 234, 274 |
| `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/ReconciliationFlow.svelte` | 278 |

**Explanation:** These files use `var(--orange, #f59e0b)`, `var(--blue, #3b82f6)`, and `var(--red, #ef4444)` as CSS custom property references. None of `--orange`, `--blue`, or `--red` are defined in `src/lib/theme/css-generator.ts` or `src/lib/theme/tokens.ts`. The theme defines `--amber` (`#f5a623`), `--green`, and `--error` but not these three. Because the primary property is always undefined, the hardcoded fallback hex fires on every render — the theme system is completely bypassed for chart and table color coding in the amortization and bank-reconciliation playgrounds.

**Suggested fix:** Add three tokens to `ThemeTokens.colors` in `tokens.ts`: `orange`, `blue`, `red`; populate them in `defaultTheme`; emit `--orange`, `--blue`, `--red` in `css-generator.ts`. Remove the raw hex fallbacks from all component stylesheets. Map semantically: `orange → --amber` equivalent (e.g. `#f59e0b`), `blue → --accent-dim` alias (or new `#3b82f6`), `red → --error-deep` alias. Centralizing via tokens enables future theme switching.

### PG-01 — Inventory Valuation Methods
**Title EN:** Inventory Valuation (FIFO · WAC · Specific ID)  
**Title FR:** Valorisation des stocks (FIFO · CMP · Identification spécifique)  
**Target learner:** Licence 2 (Financial Accounting II)

**Pedagogical objective:** Students confuse FIFO and Weighted Average Cost (WAC/CMP) and don't understand why SYSCOHADA mandates WAC as the default while IFRS allows FIFO. The playground surfaces how method choice directly changes cost of goods sold, gross margin, and closing stock value on the same set of purchases and sales.

**Core interaction:** A timeline of purchase lots (date, quantity, unit cost) and sales events. The learner selects the valuation method; the running stock card, COGS tally, and closing balance update in real time. A split-panel comparison mode shows FIFO vs WAC side-by-side on the same dataset.

**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:**
- Inputs: up to 10 purchase rows (date, qty, cost/unit), up to 8 sale rows (date, qty)
- Outputs: perpetual stock card table, total COGS, closing stock value, gross margin impact delta vs. alternative method

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry covers how to record the purchase and sale; this playground teaches *how much* to record — the valuation step that comes before the journal entry.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** CVP analyses a single product's profitability; this playground analyses the entire enterprise from published financial statements — the natural next step after students learn to prepare those statements via Journal Entry.

### PG-03 — Provisions & Contingencies Calculator
**Title EN:** Provisions & Contingencies (SYSCOHADA)  
**Title FR:** Provisions pour risques et charges  
**Target learner:** Licence 3 / practicing professional

**Pedagogical objective:** Provisions (accounts 19x in the SYSCOHADA chart) are consistently mis-classified or omitted by students. The recognition criteria (probable obligation, reliable estimate) and the adjustment journal entries (dot, reprise) are non-intuitive. There is no existing playground touching the 19x account class.

**Core interaction:** A scenario builder presents a business situation (ongoing lawsuit, warranty claims, restructuring plan). The learner must (1) decide whether to recognise a provision or disclose a contingent liability, (2) enter the estimated amount, (3) record the provision journal entry, and (4) handle the end-of-year adjustment (increase/decrease/reversal). The engine validates against SYSCOHADA recognition rules and shows the balance sheet impact.

**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:**
- Inputs: scenario type, probability estimate (certain/probable/possible/remote), estimated amount, prior year provision balance
- Outputs: recognition decision feedback, journal entry template with correct account codes (e.g. 6815 / 191), balance sheet delta, narrative explanation of SYSCOHADA vs IFRS IAS 37 treatment

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Bank Reconciliation and Journal Entry cover routine transactions; provisions represent a judgment-based accrual with regulatory nuance that no existing playground addresses.

### PG-04 — TVA / VAT Input–Output Reconciliation
**Title EN:** VAT Input–Output Reconciliation  
**Title FR:** Déclaration TVA — taxe collectée vs taxe déductible  
**Target learner:** Licence 2 / practicing professional

**Pedagogical objective:** In OHADA-zone countries VAT (TVA) rates vary by country (18 % Côte d'Ivoire, 19.25 % Cameroon, 20 % Senegal, etc.) and students struggle to compute the net tax payable, identify non-deductible input VAT, and reconcile the declaration. No existing playground covers indirect taxes.

**Core interaction:** A month's purchase and sales register. Each row has an amount, a VAT rate (selectable per OHADA-country), and a deductibility flag. The playground computes: output VAT collected, deductible input VAT, non-deductible input VAT, net VAT payable or credit. Displays the statutory declaration form layout (Déclaration de TVA) as the output.

**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:**
- Inputs: country selector (drives default rate), up to 12 purchase rows, up to 12 sales rows, each with amount + rate + deductibility toggle
- Outputs: output VAT total, deductible input total, non-deductible input, net balance, journal entry for VAT payable (445x accounts)

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Connects to Journal Entry (which covers recording transactions) by adding the indirect-tax layer that every OHADA business transaction carries.

### PG-05 — Lease vs. Buy Decision Analyser
**Title EN:** Lease vs. Buy Decision (NPV method)  
**Title FR:** Crédit-bail vs. acquisition — comparaison par VAN  
**Target learner:** Master / Finance professional

**Pedagogical objective:** Students applying TVM concepts cannot compare leasing and outright purchase because they conflate cash flow timing, residual values, and tax shields. SYSCOHADA's treatment of finance leases (crédit-bail) in the off-balance-sheet notes adds a layer of complexity not covered by the Amortization playground.

**Core interaction:** Two side-by-side panels: Buy (loan financed) and Lease. The learner enters asset price, useful life, loan rate, lease payment schedule, tax rate, and discount rate. The engine computes the NPV of after-tax cash flows under each scenario, the break-even discount rate (indifference point), and a sensitivity chart (NPV difference vs. discount rate). Integrates `src/lib/finance/pmt.ts` and `src/lib/finance/irr.ts` already in the codebase.

**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:**
- Inputs: asset cost, useful life (years), loan interest rate, lease annual payment, residual value, corporate tax rate, cost of capital
- Outputs: NPV of buy vs. lease, advantage delta, indifference rate, annual cash flow comparison table, SYSCOHADA balance sheet treatment note (crédit-bail capitalisation threshold)

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** TVM teaches individual cash flow discounting; Amortization covers loan repayment. This playground combines both in a real capital budgeting decision, bridging business-math and financial-accounting categories.
