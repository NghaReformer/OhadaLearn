### Playground A — Inventory Valuation (FIFO vs. CUMP)

**EN:** Inventory Valuation Methods  
**FR:** Méthodes de valorisation des stocks

**Target learner:** Licence 2 / Licence 3

**Pedagogical objective:** SYSCOHADA mandates the Coût Unitaire Moyen Pondéré (CUMP / weighted-average) method, while IFRS also permits FIFO. Students conflate the two, over-report ending inventory under CUMP, or mis-sequence when to recalculate the moving average. This playground makes the gap viscerally visible.

**Core interaction:** The learner adds purchase and sale transactions to a running stock card. Two side-by-side tables compute closing inventory cost simultaneously under FIFO and CUMP. Cells that differ are highlighted; a "SYSCOHADA compliance" badge locks in CUMP as the reportable figure.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: quantity purchased, unit cost, quantity sold (date-ordered)
- Outputs: running CUMP after each purchase, FIFO layers, cost of goods sold (both methods), ending inventory value, difference in profit impact

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry teaches double-entry mechanics; this teaches *what value* flows into the stock debit entry — a prerequisite for correct financial statements under SYSCOHADA.

### Playground B — VAT / TVA Accounting Entries

**EN:** VAT Accounting (Input Tax Credit)  
**FR:** Comptabilité de la TVA (TVA déductible / collectée)

**Target learner:** Licence 2 / practicing professional

**Pedagogical objective:** Students confuse VAT as an expense vs. a pass-through liability. They fail to separate TVA déductible (account 445) from TVA collectée (account 443) and miscalculate net TVA payable. The OHADA zone has multiple VAT rates (18 % standard in most member states, reduced rates for essentials), adding variety.

**Core interaction:** The learner enters a set of purchase and sale invoices with amounts and applicable VAT rate. The playground auto-generates the journal entries in SYSCOHADA accounts (class 4), sums the two sides, and computes net VAT payable or credit. A "filing period" toggle aggregates across a month.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: supplier invoices (HT amount, VAT rate), customer invoices (HT amount, VAT rate), reporting period
- Outputs: TVA déductible total, TVA collectée total, net VAT payable (or credit), journal entries in SYSCOHADA accounts 4451/4455/4456

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry playground uses generic accounts; this teaches the specific class-4 VAT accounts that every SYSCOHADA practitioner encounters daily. Bank Reconciliation playground handles cash flows but not the underlying tax entries.

### Playground C — Financial Statement Preparation

**EN:** Trial Balance to Financial Statements  
**FR:** De la balance de vérification aux états financiers SYSCOHADA

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master

**Pedagogical objective:** Students who can post journal entries often freeze when asked to reclassify a trial balance into the SYSCOHADA *état financier* format (Bilan + Compte de résultat + Tableau des flux de trésorerie). Common mistakes: placing an asset credit balance on the liability side, misidentifying non-current vs. current assets, forgetting the profit-transfer entry.

**Core interaction:** The learner receives a completed adjusted trial balance (pre-loaded from a scenario). They drag-and-drop each account row into the correct slot of the Bilan or Compte de résultat. Debits and credits auto-total, a running balance-check indicator turns green when the sheets balance. A "reveal" mode shows the model answer with per-account explanations.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: a given trial balance (from scenario YAML); learner classification drag gestures
- Outputs: SYSCOHADA-formatted Bilan, Compte de résultat, balance-check indicator (assets = liabilities + equity), score on classification accuracy

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** This is the natural successor to Journal Entry — it teaches what to do *after* the entries are posted. It also uses the `scenarios` architecture already in place.

### Playground D — Foreign Currency Transactions

**EN:** Foreign Exchange Accounting (SYSCOHADA)  
**FR:** Comptabilisation des opérations en devises

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master / professional

**Pedagogical objective:** SYSCOHADA Chapter on monetary items requires translation at closing rate with exchange differences booked to "Écarts de conversion" (accounts 476/477) — a treatment distinct from IFRS IAS 21. Students miss the two-step mechanism: initial recognition at transaction rate, then year-end adjustment.

**Core interaction:** The learner enters a foreign-currency receivable or payable (amount, currency, transaction date, transaction rate). The playground shows the initial journal entry. The learner then sets a "closing date" rate; the playground computes the exchange difference and prompts the learner to post the adjustment entry to 476 or 477, explaining which direction (gain vs. loss) goes to which account.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: original foreign amount, currency pair, transaction rate, closing rate
- Outputs: initial recognition entry (at transaction rate), closing-rate adjustment, account 476 or 477 debit/credit, impact on net assets
- Uses the existing `format/` currency utilities and the 16-currency list already in `$lib/format`

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** TVM and Interest handle time value; this handles *currency* value — a topic absent from all current playgrounds despite being tested in every SYSCOHADA exam.

### Playground E — Payroll Journal Entries

**EN:** Payroll Accounting (SYSCOHADA)  
**FR:** Comptabilisation de la paie

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / practicing professional

**Pedagogical objective:** Payroll entries in SYSCOHADA use class-4 and class-6 accounts that students rarely see in introductory courses. Common errors: booking gross salary to expense instead of net; conflating employee social charges (subtracted from gross) with employer charges (added on top); ignoring the liability accounts for social-body payables.

**Core interaction:** The learner inputs gross salary, employee social charge rate, income tax withholding, and employer charge rate. The playground generates: (1) the gross-to-net payslip table; (2) the payroll journal entry in SYSCOHADA accounts (661 Rémunérations, 431/432 Social, 421 Personnel, 447 Withholding); (3) the employer charge entry (charges 664, crediting 431/432). An "explain this line" tooltip on each account.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: gross salary, employee SS rate, income tax rate, employer charge rate (can vary by country within OHADA zone — Mali 24 %, Senegal 22 %, etc.)
- Outputs: net salary, total payroll cost to employer, full set of journal entries in SYSCOHADA accounts, breakdown of each debit/credit
- The currency formatter handles FCFA natively via existing `$lib/format`

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** No current playground touches class-6 expense accounts or class-4 social/tax payables in a structured exercise. This fills a prominent gap between theory and the day-one job of a junior accountant in the OHADA zone.
