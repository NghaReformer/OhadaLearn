### P1 — Working Capital & Cash Conversion Cycle

**EN:** Working Capital Simulator | **FR:** Simulateur de Fonds de Roulement

**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master 1 — financial analysis modules

**Pedagogical objective:** Students routinely confuse *working capital* (fonds de roulement, FR), *working capital requirement* (besoin en fonds de roulement, BFR), and *net cash* (trésorerie nette). This playground makes the three-way identity `FR = BFR + TN` visceral and manipulable.

**Core interaction:** Slider-driven balance sheet with live FR/BFR/TN readout and a traffic-light indicator (green = positive TN, red = overdraft). A second panel shows the cash conversion cycle days (DIO + DSO − DPO) and an animated timeline.

**Minimum viable inputs:** Inventory (stock), accounts receivable, accounts payable, fixed assets, long-term debt, equity. **Outputs:** FR, BFR, trésorerie nette, CCC in days, short interpretation sentence.

**Why it fits:** TVM and amortization handle long-term finance; this fills the short-term liquidity gap central to SYSCOHADA SME analysis coursework.

---

### P2 — VAT Ledger & Tax Return Builder

**EN:** VAT Ledger & Return | **FR:** Journal de TVA et Déclaration

**Target learner:** Licence 2–3, practicing bookkeepers in OHADA zone

**Pedagogical objective:** Students confuse collectible VAT (TVA collectée, account 4431) with deductible VAT (TVA déductible, account 4452) and struggle to derive the net payable. The SYSCOHADA chart of accounts uses specific account codes that differ from IFRS contexts.

**Core interaction:** A transaction ledger where the learner categorises each line as a purchase or sale, selects a VAT rate (0%, 10%, 18%, 19.25% — covering Cameroon, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire variants), and the playground auto-posts to TVA accounts and builds a draft monthly return form (Déclaration de TVA).

**Minimum viable inputs:** 5–10 pre-loaded transactions (editable), VAT rate selector per transaction. **Outputs:** TVA collectée total, TVA déductible total, net TVA à décaisser / à récupérer, journal entries for the closing entry.

**Why it fits:** The journal-entry playground teaches debits/credits generically; this applies that skill to the most common OHADA compliance task.

### P3 — Financial Ratio Dashboard

**EN:** Financial Ratio Analyzer | **FR:** Tableau de Bord des Ratios Financiers

**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master 1 — financial analysis or audit track

**Pedagogical objective:** Ratio analysis is taught as a formula list. Students never develop intuition for how one lever (e.g., faster receivables collection) cascades across liquidity, leverage, and profitability ratios simultaneously.

**Core interaction:** A simplified income statement + balance sheet with editable cells. Changing any cell instantly recomputes a 12-ratio dashboard grouped by SYSCOHADA categories (liquidité, solvabilité, rentabilité, activité). A "what-if" mode lets students set a target ratio and highlights which inputs need to move.

**Minimum viable inputs:** Revenue, COGS, operating expenses, current assets, current liabilities, total debt, equity. **Outputs:** Current ratio, quick ratio, debt/equity, ROE, ROA, asset turnover, operating margin, gross margin, EBITDA margin, receivables days, payables days, inventory days.

**Why it fits:** CVP handles cost-volume decisions; this handles post-hoc financial health assessment — the next step in the same course sequence.

### P4 — Lease vs. Buy Decision Model (SYSCOHADA IAS 17 vs. IFRS 16)

**EN:** Lease vs. Buy Analyzer | **FR:** Simulateur Location / Acquisition

**Target learner:** Master 1–2, corporate finance or advanced accounting

**Pedagogical objective:** SYSCOHADA still references IAS 17 classification (operating vs. finance lease) while IFRS 16 requires full on-balance-sheet recognition. Students preparing for DSCG or DEC exams must understand both regimes and the NPV decision boundary.

**Core interaction:** Toggle between IAS 17 and IFRS 16 treatment. Inputs: asset cost, lease term, annual payment, residual value, discount rate, tax rate. Outputs: NAL (net advantage to leasing), balance-sheet impact under each standard, year-by-year cash flow table, break-even discount rate.

**Minimum viable inputs:** Asset cost, lease payments (annual), term (years), discount rate, corporate tax rate. **Outputs:** NAL, present value of lease payments, present value of ownership costs, recommendation banner.

**Why it fits:** Amortization playground covers asset write-down; this adds the acquisition-mode decision that precedes it — filling a gap in the asset-lifecycle coverage.

### P5 — Consolidation Eliminations Trainer

**EN:** Group Consolidation Trainer | **FR:** Entraîneur aux Retraitements de Consolidation

**Target learner:** Master 2, CPA preparation, group finance roles

**Pedagogical objective:** Intercompany eliminations (intra-group sales, dividends, unrealised profit on stock) are the hardest consolidation step. Students learn the theory but cannot practice without a full group dataset. This playground provides a minimal 2-entity group and guides the learner through each elimination journal entry step-by-step.

**Core interaction:** A parent + one subsidiary with pre-loaded trial balances. The learner is prompted to identify each intra-group item, enter the elimination journal entry, and submit. The playground validates each entry and shows the consolidated balance sheet building in real time.

**Minimum viable inputs:** Parent and subsidiary individual trial balances, intercompany sales amount, intercompany dividend, unrealised profit percentage. **Outputs:** Elimination journal entries (validated), consolidated income statement, consolidated balance sheet.

**Why it fits:** The journal-entry playground develops the mechanics; this applies them to the most advanced SYSCOHADA topic — completing the skill ladder from individual accounts to group accounts.
