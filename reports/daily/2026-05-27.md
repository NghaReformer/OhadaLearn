### Playground A — Ratio Analysis Dashboard
**EN:** Financial Ratio Analysis | **FR:** Analyse des Ratios Financiers

**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master 1 — students transitioning from recording transactions to interpreting financial statements.

**Pedagogical objective:** Most students can compute a ratio from a formula sheet but cannot explain what it signals or why it deteriorates. This playground closes the gap between calculation and diagnosis by making the relationship between balance-sheet/income-statement line items and five ratio families (liquidity, solvency, profitability, activity, market) viscerally clear.

**Core interaction:** The learner adjusts individual SYSCOHADA balance-sheet and income-statement cells on a simplified two-period comparative statement. Every change instantly updates a dashboard of 10–12 ratios with colour-coded trend arrows and a plain-language interpretation beneath each ratio ("Your current ratio dropped below 1.0 — the company cannot cover short-term obligations from current assets alone.").

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: ~12 editable numeric cells (current assets, current liabilities, total debt, equity, revenue, COGS, net income, receivables, inventory)
- Outputs: computed ratio grid, trend arrows (period-over-period), traffic-light colours, one-sentence SYSCOHADA-aligned interpretation per ratio, a downloadable CSV

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** `cvp` focuses on break-even; `tvm` and `amortization` focus on cash flows over time. None teach the *reading* of a balance sheet in the OHADA plan comptable structure.

### Playground B — Chart of Accounts Journal Trainer
**EN:** Guided Double-Entry Trainer | **FR:** Entraîneur à la Partie Double Guidé**

**Target learner:** Licence 1 — students who just learned the debit/credit rule and need repetition with SYSCOHADA account classes.

**Pedagogical objective:** `journal-entry` already exists but does not provide scaffolded feedback. This playground presents realistic OHADA-zone business scenarios (e.g. "Purchased office supplies on credit, 150 000 FCFA") and asks the learner to select accounts from the SYSCOHADA chart, assign amounts, and balance the entry — then scores the attempt with itemised feedback rather than just correct/incorrect.

**Core interaction:** Step-by-step scenario card → learner picks debit account(s) from a filtered chart-of-accounts typeahead (reusing `AccountPicker`) → picks credit account(s) → enters amounts → submits. The grader compares against a canonical answer and explains each discrepancy with the relevant SYSCOHADA rule number.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: scenario card (text), account-picker rows (×2–4), amount fields
- Outputs: score (0–100), per-line verdict, SYSCOHADA rule reference, "try again" with hint tier

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** The existing `journal-entry` playground is a free-form recorder; this is a *teaching* module with correctness feedback. It also exercises the shared `AccountPicker` component and `grading/` module already in `src/lib/`.

### Playground C — Cash-Flow Statement Builder
**EN:** Cash Flow Statement Builder | **FR:** Constructeur du Tableau des Flux de Trésorerie

**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master — students preparing for SYSCOHADA financial statement preparation.

**Pedagogical objective:** The indirect method for the operating section (reconciling net income to cash) is one of the most confusion-generating topics in SYSCOHADA accounting. Students mix up the sign conventions for adding back depreciation, adjusting for working-capital changes, and separating investing/financing flows.

**Core interaction:** The learner is given a pre-populated income statement and comparative balance sheet. They drag-and-drop each line item into one of three buckets (Operating / Investing / Financing) and specify the sign adjustment. The playground validates placement against SYSCOHADA TFT (Tableau des Flux de Trésorerie) rules and shows the running total per section, flagging sign errors with an explanation.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: ~15 line items (draggable), sign toggle per item (+/−), free-entry for adjustments not on the pre-populated list
- Outputs: TFT preview (auto-built from learner's choices), per-item correctness badge, net cash reconciliation check, final score

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** `amortization` covers the depreciation *calculation*; this playground covers how depreciation flows into the cash-flow statement — a common exam failure point.

### Playground D — Break-Even Sensitivity (Multi-Product)
**EN:** Multi-Product Break-Even Sensitivity | **FR:** Seuil de Rentabilité Multi-Produits**

**Target learner:** Licence 2–3, practicing SME accountants in the OHADA zone.

**Pedagogical objective:** The existing `cvp` playground handles a single-product scenario. Real OHADA-zone SMEs (e.g. small distributors common in Cameroon/Côte d'Ivoire) sell multiple products with different margins. Students need to understand weighted-average contribution margin and how product-mix shifts change break-even — a concept not covered by the current CVP module.

**Core interaction:** The learner adds up to 5 products with unit price, variable cost, and projected sales-mix %. A live chart shows the composite break-even point and how it shifts when the learner drags a mix-percentage slider. A second panel shows the margin of safety and operating leverage for the current mix.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: product table (name, price, variable cost, mix %, up to 5 rows), fixed costs
- Outputs: weighted contribution margin ratio, composite break-even units/revenue, chart (revenue vs. total cost), margin of safety %, operating leverage factor

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Direct extension of `cvp` without duplication — it occupies a distinct learner level (multi-product) and surface area (mix sensitivity) that `cvp` explicitly does not cover.

### Playground E — VAT & Tax Ledger Simulator
**EN:** VAT & Tax Ledger Simulator | **FR:** Simulateur de TVA et de Déclaration Fiscale

**Target learner:** Licence 2, practicing bookkeepers in OHADA-member states (especially Cameroon, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire).

**Pedagogical objective:** VAT accounting in the OHADA zone uses specific SYSCOHADA accounts (4431 TVA collectée, 4452 TVA déductible sur ABS) and the periodic netting procedure. Students and junior bookkeepers routinely confuse the direction of VAT entries, the timing of deductibility, and the monthly declaration. No existing playground covers tax ledger accounts.

**Core interaction:** The learner enters a set of monthly sales and purchase transactions (with VAT rates varying 0–19.25% by item type, per CEMAC/UEMOA rates). The playground posts the corresponding journal entries automatically and shows the running 4431/4452 account balances. At month-end, the learner clicks "Compute declaration" and sees the net VAT payable/refundable, which they must validate before "filing." Incorrect VAT account selection on any line is flagged immediately.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: transaction rows (type: sale/purchase, amount HT, VAT rate selector), month selector
- Outputs: auto-posted journal, account balances (4431, 4452, 4455 variants), net declaration amount, penalty simulation for late filing
- Locale-aware formatting via `src/lib/format/` (FCFA, XOF/XAF)

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** No existing playground touches tax-account mechanics. This directly addresses a documented skill gap for practicing professionals in OHADA-member states — a core B2B2C use case for OhadaLearn — and exercises the existing `format/` currency system with multi-rate VAT scenarios.
