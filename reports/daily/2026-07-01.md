#### H-1 — Undefined CSS custom properties `--orange`, `--blue`, `--red` used across 9+ files

**Severity:** High  
**Files (sample):**
- `src/lib/components/playground/KpiStrip.svelte:76`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/amortization/components/ChartPanel.svelte:436,444,493,501,515,529,533`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/amortization/components/ScheduleTable.svelte:240,246,259`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/amortization/components/InputsPanel.svelte:604,605`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/VarianceScale.svelte:230,260,261,331,335,370,416`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/ScenarioWalkthrough.svelte:234,274`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/TransactionJournal.svelte:382,383`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/ReconciliationFlow.svelte:278`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/LedgerPanel.svelte:175`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/BankStatementPanel.svelte:175`

**Explanation:** `css-generator.ts` emits `--green`, `--amber`, `--error`, and `--accent` among others — but never `--orange`, `--blue`, or `--red`. These variables are undefined at runtime, so the hex fallback in every `var(--orange, #f59e0b)` / `var(--blue, #3b82f6)` / `var(--red, #ef4444)` is **always** used. The fallbacks are Tailwind palette values, not the design-system tokens (e.g. `--amber` is `#f5a623`, not `#f59e0b`; `--error` is `#f06070`, not `#ef4444`). Theme changes will never reach these components.

**Suggested fix:** Replace with the nearest defined token:
- `--orange` → `--amber`
- `--blue` → `--accent`
- `--red` → `--error`

Update `css-generator.ts` if semantic aliases are preferred.

### MEDIUM

#### M-1 — Client component imports from `$lib/server/db/types`

**Severity:** Medium  
**Files:**
- `src/lib/components/feedback/FeedbackForm.svelte:7`
- `src/routes/api/feedback/validation.ts:1-5`

**Explanation:** `FeedbackForm.svelte` uses `import type { FeedbackType, FeedbackSeverity } from '$lib/server/db/types'`. Although TypeScript erases `import type` at compile time, SvelteKit's Vite plugin enforces `$lib/server` boundaries by static import analysis and may fail the build. Even if it passes today, it violates the architectural contract. `validation.ts` (a route co-location file) also imports from `$lib/server/db/types`, making it subtly server-coupled.

**Suggested fix:** Move the shared type-only definitions (`FeedbackType`, `FeedbackSeverity`) out of `$lib/server/db/types.ts` into a new `$lib/types/feedback.ts` (or inline them in the component). Server-only code (Supabase calls, DB schemas) stays under `$lib/server/`.

### Idea 1 — Inventory Valuation Methods

**Title (EN):** Inventory Costing Methods  
**Title (FR):** Méthodes d'évaluation des stocks  
**Target learner:** Licence 2, practicing accountant  
**Pedagogical objective:** SYSCOHADA mandates the weighted-average cost (CMP) method for inventory valuation, yet students regularly confuse it with FIFO and sometimes erroneously apply LIFO (not permitted under SYSCOHADA). This playground fixes the misconception by computing all three side-by-side and highlighting why CMP is the required method.  
**Core interaction:** The learner enters a sequence of inventory movements (purchases with unit cost, sales quantities). The engine computes COGS and ending inventory under FIFO, CMP, and a greyed-out LIFO column. A live difference banner shows XAF impact on net income.  
**Minimum viable inputs:** Initial stock (qty + unit cost), up to 10 purchase/sale transactions with dates, quantities, and unit costs.  
**Outputs:** Inventory ledger table per method, COGS total, ending inventory value, income difference table, SYSCOHADA compliance flag.  
**Complement:** Fills a gap between the amortization playground (asset cost allocation) and the journal-entry playground (recording movements). Reuses `format/currency.ts` and the `AccountPicker` component for stock accounts.

### Idea 2 — VAT / TVA Computation and Return

**Title (EN):** VAT Calculation & Return  
**Title (FR):** Calcul de TVA et déclaration  
**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master, practicing accountant  
**Pedagogical objective:** Students struggle to distinguish taxable base from tax amounts, to apply reverse-charge mechanisms, and to compute net TVA payable (output TVA minus deductible input TVA). The OHADA zone has heterogeneous VAT rates (18 % in Cameroon, 19.25 % in Côte d'Ivoire, etc.) adding extra confusion.  
**Core interaction:** The learner enters a list of sales invoices and purchase invoices for a reporting period, selects the country/rate, and classifies each line as standard-rated, exempt, or zero-rated. The engine builds the TVA return form and computes the net amount due or refundable.  
**Minimum viable inputs:** Country (drives the rate), up to 15 invoices per direction with HT amount and taxability class.  
**Outputs:** TVA return summary (output TVA, deductible input TVA, net balance), journal entries per SYSCOHADA plan (4441/4455 accounts), common error highlights.  
**Complement:** Bridges the gap between the journal-entry playground (recording mechanics) and real-world tax compliance. Uses `format/currency.ts` with FCFA-zone currencies and `AccountPicker` with SYSCOHADA account codes.

### Idea 3 — Payroll / Bulletin de Paie

**Title (EN):** Payroll Calculator  
**Title (FR):** Simulateur de Bulletin de Paie  
**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master, HR/finance practitioner  
**Pedagogical objective:** Payroll in the OHADA zone (CNPS contributions, IRPP withholding, regional levies) is taught abstractly. Students cannot map gross salary to employer cost and cannot produce the SYSCOHADA journal entries (641, 431, 447). This playground makes the full gross-to-net calculation transparent.  
**Core interaction:** The learner enters gross salary, employee category, family charges, and selects the OHADA country. Sliders let them experiment with gross salary to see IRPP brackets apply progressively. The output shows the payslip breakdown and the four journal entries an employer must post.  
**Minimum viable inputs:** Country, gross salary, employee category (cadre/non-cadre), number of dependants.  
**Outputs:** Net-to-gross bridge (CNPS employee, CNPS employer, IRPP by bracket, CAC/RAV where applicable), payslip PDF-ready layout, SYSCOHADA journal entries.  
**Complement:** The most requested real-world topic not yet covered. Reuses journal entry display components and the `fmtCurrency` formatter for FCFA amounts.

### Idea 4 — Financial Ratio Analysis

**Title (EN):** Financial Ratio Dashboard  
**Title (FR):** Tableau de Bord des Ratios Financiers  
**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master  
**Pedagogical objective:** Students can compute individual ratios but don't understand how ratios interact or what thresholds signal distress under SYSCOHADA's TAFIRE framework. This playground makes ratio families visible simultaneously and ties them to the SYSCOHADA balance sheet structure.  
**Core interaction:** The learner fills in a simplified SYSCOHADA balance sheet (actif/passif) and income statement. The dashboard instantly computes and colour-codes 12 ratios across four families (liquidity, solvency, profitability, activity), with sparklines showing industry benchmarks for OHADA-zone SMEs.  
**Minimum viable inputs:** 8-10 key balance-sheet line items (BFR, trésorerie, fonds propres, total bilan, CA, résultat net).  
**Outputs:** Ratio tiles with traffic-light status, radar chart across families, one-sentence interpretation per ratio, TAFIRE-specific indicators where applicable.  
**Complement:** Synthesis playground that draws on concepts from all existing modules. Uses the existing `KpiStrip` component and the chart utilities already in `cvp/chart-utils.ts`.

### Idea 5 — Depreciation Method Comparison (SYSCOHADA vs IFRS)

**Title (EN):** Depreciation Method Comparison  
**Title (FR):** Comparaison des Méthodes d'Amortissement  
**Target learner:** Licence 2/3, Master  
**Pedagogical objective:** The existing static HTML depreciation playground shows one method in isolation. Students confuse straight-line (linéaire), declining-balance (dégressif fiscal), and units-of-production (UP) methods, and don't understand how SYSCOHADA differs from IFRS on component depreciation and residual values.  
**Core interaction:** Side-by-side schedule tables for straight-line, declining-balance, and units-of-production. A toggle switches between SYSCOHADA and IFRS component depreciation rules. Chart overlays show the NBV curve divergence between methods.  
**Minimum viable inputs:** Asset cost, residual value, useful life, declining-balance rate, annual production/hours (for UP method).  
**Outputs:** Three parallel amortization tables, carrying-value comparison chart, total depreciation reconciliation, difference in net income per year.  
**Complement:** Directly replaces/supersedes the static `depreciation-playground.html` with a native Svelte module, integrating with the amortization playground's existing `engine.ts` and `ScheduleTable` components.
