### Playground 1 — Inventory Valuation Methods
**EN:** Inventory Valuation Methods | **FR:** Méthodes d'évaluation des stocks

**Target learner:** Licence 2 / Licence 3

**Pedagogical objective:** Students consistently confuse FIFO (PEPS in French) and CMUP (weighted average cost) outcomes. The misconception is that the choice of method is cosmetic — this playground reveals the direct income statement and balance sheet impact of the same physical stock movements evaluated under each method.

**Core interaction:** Learner enters a series of purchase lots (date, quantity, unit cost) and sales events. The playground computes the stock ledger, COGS, and closing inventory value simultaneously under PEPS and CMUP, showing side-by-side variance when prices are moving.

**Minimum viable inputs:** 4–8 transaction rows (purchase/sale toggle, quantity, unit price). **Outputs:** stock ledger table per method, COGS per method, closing inventory per method, gross profit impact delta.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry covers the mechanics of recording stock movements; this playground addresses the *valuation* dimension that SYSCOHADA OHADA Acte uniforme (AUDCG) prescribes. It naturally pairs with the depreciation playground (both are SYSCOHADA asset measurement topics).

### Playground 2 — TVA (VAT) Computation
**EN:** VAT Computation (OHADA Zone) | **FR:** Calcul de la TVA (Zone OHADA)

**Target learner:** Licence 1 / Licence 2, and practicing SME bookkeepers

**Pedagogical objective:** The OHADA-zone standard rate of 18% (Cameroun, Côte d'Ivoire, Sénégal, etc.) creates confusion between TVA collectée (output tax), TVA déductible (input tax on purchases), credit de TVA, and TVA à décaisser. Most students cannot correctly determine the net liability before their first internship.

**Core interaction:** Learner records sales invoices (HT amount, tax rate — including exemptions and reduced rates) and purchase invoices. The playground computes the TVA declaration: collectée − déductible = à décaisser (or crédit). Toggle to show the correct journal entries for each line.

**Minimum viable inputs:** Up to 10 invoice lines (sales/purchase, HT amount, rate selector: 18% / 0% exempt / reduced). **Outputs:** TVA collectée total, TVA déductible total, net position with sign (amount due / credit), corresponding journal entry.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** No existing playground covers indirect taxation. This is the single most common calculation a Licence 1 student will perform in a practicum — before amortization, before bank reconciliation. High pedagogical ROI.

### Playground 3 — Trial Balance → Financial Statements
**EN:** Trial Balance Workbook | **FR:** Balance de vérification → États financiers

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master 1

**Pedagogical objective:** Students who can post individual entries still struggle to close books and produce the four SYSCOHADA statements (Bilan, Compte de résultat, Tableau de flux simplifié, État annexé excerpt). The misconception is that producing financial statements is merely sorting accounts — the closing and reclassification steps are invisible to them.

**Core interaction:** Learner is given a pre-populated adjusted trial balance (editable amounts). The playground maps each account to the correct SYSCOHADA statement line using the plan comptable OHADA. Learner drags/assigns any unlabeled accounts, then the playground assembles the Bilan and Compte de résultat in SYSCOHADA column layout, highlighting balancing errors.

**Minimum viable inputs:** 15–25 pre-seeded trial balance rows (account code, debit/credit balance). **Outputs:** SYSCOHADA-format Bilan (Actif / Passif), Compte de résultat, residual balance check (must equal zero), colour-coded mapping of each trial balance row to its statement.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry teaches *recording*; this playground teaches *aggregating and reporting* — the capstone step. It directly consumes the chart-of-accounts data already in `src/lib/shared/chart-of-accounts/ohada-accounts.ts`.

### Playground 4 — Foreign Currency Transactions
**EN:** Foreign Currency Transactions | **FR:** Opérations en devises étrangères

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master 1 / practicing professional

**Pedagogical objective:** SYSCOHADA Article 37 requires monetary items to be retranslated at closing rate. Students confuse transaction-date recording, balance-sheet-date retranslation, and settlement-date differences — treating exchange differences as errors rather than income/expense.

**Core interaction:** Learner enters a foreign-currency transaction (currency, amount, transaction date rate), then retranslates it at a learner-specified balance sheet date rate, and finally settles at a settlement rate. The playground computes the exchange difference at each step, classifies it as financial income or expense, and shows the journal entries for all three events.

**Minimum viable inputs:** Currency selector (USD, EUR, GBP, CNY), transaction amount, three exchange rates (transaction / closing / settlement). **Outputs:** FCFA-equivalent at each date, exchange difference per period, journal entries in SYSCOHADA account codes (476/477 accounts), P&L impact.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Complements the Interest playground (multi-currency financing) and prepares Master students for SYSCOHADA consolidation, which the platform does not yet cover.

### Playground 5 — Flexible Budget & Variance Analysis
**EN:** Flexible Budget & Variance Analysis | **FR:** Budget flexible et analyse des écarts

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master 1 (managerial accounting track)

**Pedagogical objective:** Students confuse planning variances (volume effect) with efficiency variances (cost effect). The CVP playground shows break-even analysis at a fixed volume; this playground addresses *why actual results differ from budget* — a gap in the managerial-accounting coverage.

**Core interaction:** Learner inputs a static budget (planned volume, planned unit variable cost, planned fixed cost) and actual results (actual volume, actual variable cost, actual fixed cost). The playground decomposes the total profit variance into: sales volume variance, selling price variance, variable cost efficiency variance, fixed cost spending variance — displayed as a waterfall chart.

**Minimum viable inputs:** 6 fields (budgeted volume, actual volume, budgeted VC/unit, actual VC/unit, budgeted FC, actual FC). **Outputs:** Static budget profit, flexible budget profit, actual profit, four labelled variance tiles (favourable / adverse), waterfall bridge chart.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** CVP covers planning; this covers control. Together they complete the managerial-accounting arc from pricing decisions through budget execution monitoring. The waterfall chart is a distinct visualisation type not yet used in any playground.
