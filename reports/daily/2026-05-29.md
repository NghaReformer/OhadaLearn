### P-1 · Inventory Valuation Methods
**EN:** Inventory Valuation Methods
**FR:** Méthodes de Valorisation des Stocks

**Target learner:** Licence 1/2

**Pedagogical objective:** Students routinely confuse how FIFO, AVCO (coût moyen pondéré), and LIFO affect reported cost of goods sold and ending stock value — and therefore gross profit. The misconception is that "the method only changes paperwork"; this playground makes the monetary impact visible and visceral.

**Core interaction:** The learner builds a simple stock card by entering purchase lines (date, quantity, unit cost) and sales lines (date, quantity). They then toggle between FIFO / AVCO / LIFO and watch the fiche de stock and COGS recalculate in real time. A side-by-side comparison panel shows gross profit under each method.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: Initial stock (qty + cost), up to 10 movement lines (purchase or sale, date, qty, unit cost)
- Outputs: Per-method fiche de stock (tableau), COGS, ending inventory value, gross profit, automatically generated journal entries for each sale (SYSCOHADA accounts 601/371)

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** The journal-entry playground teaches debit/credit mechanics; this playground gives meaning to the amounts that flow into account 601 (Achats de marchandises) and 371 (Stocks de marchandises). Together they complete the merchandising accounting cycle.

### P-2 · Payroll Calculation Simulator
**EN:** Payroll Calculation Simulator
**FR:** Simulateur de Bulletin de Paie

**Target learner:** Licence 2/3, practicing HR/accounting professional

**Pedagogical objective:** OHADA-zone students frequently misunderstand the split between gross salary, employee social charges (CNSS part salariale), income tax (IRPP), and employer charges (CNSS part patronale). Each OHADA member state has its own charge rates; this is not covered by generic textbooks.

**Core interaction:** Learner enters a gross monthly salary, selects a country (Cameroon, Ivory Coast, Senegal, DR Congo — configurable), and provides family situation (single / married, number of dependents). The playground computes: net salary, employee deductions (CNSS salariale, IRPP), employer charges (CNSS patronale, accident du travail levy), total payroll cost, and the corresponding SYSCOHADA payroll journal entry (accounts 641, 421, 431, 447).

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: Gross salary, country selector, family quota (pour IRPP)
- Outputs: Annotated pay slip (bulletin), employer total cost, breakdown table, SYSCOHADA journal entry

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** The journal-entry playground has abstract scenarios; real payroll transactions are among the most frequent entries a graduate will handle. This playground bridges theory directly to a tangible monthly workflow.

### P-3 · Cash Flow Statement Builder
**EN:** Cash Flow Statement (Indirect Method)
**FR:** Tableau des Flux de Trésorerie — Méthode Indirecte

**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master 1/2

**Pedagogical objective:** The indirect method of building a TFT from net income confuses most Licence 3 students because it requires re-adding non-cash charges and adjusting for working capital changes — the signs are counter-intuitive (receivables increase → cash outflow). Students need to manipulate the inputs to feel the direction of each adjustment before it becomes intuitive.

**Core interaction:** Learner inputs two simplified SYSCOHADA balance sheets (N-1 and N) plus the compte de résultat net income figure. The playground derives: operating cash flow (via indirect method), investing cash flow (from non-current asset changes), financing cash flow (from debt/equity changes), and reconciles to the net change in cash. Each line item is annotated with why its sign is positive or negative.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: Net income, D&A charge, Δ receivables, Δ inventory, Δ payables, capex/disposals, debt drawdown/repayment, dividends paid
- Outputs: SYSCOHADA-formatted TFT (three-section), highlighted sign-reversal annotations, free cash flow summary KPI

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** TVM and amortization deal with individual cash flows; this playground teaches the aggregated, statutory view of cash generation — the most important document in a credit or audit context.

### P-4 · VAT Return Simulator
**EN:** VAT Return Simulator
**FR:** Simulateur de Déclaration de TVA

**Target learner:** Licence 2, practicing accountant/tax officer

**Pedagogical objective:** Students know TVA is 19.25% in Cameroon but cannot articulate the deductibility rules, the reversal entries for TVA sur achats, or how a crédit de TVA arises and is recovered. The declaratory process is almost never taught with real numbers.

**Core interaction:** Learner enters sales invoices (HT amount, applicable TVA rate, date) and purchase invoices (HT amount, deductibility percentage, date). The playground accumulates TVA collectée and TVA déductible, computes the net position, and generates either a TVA payable journal entry (account 4441) or a TVA crédit entry (account 4449). A monthly/quarterly timeline shows the carry-forward of credits.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: 1–15 sales lines + 1–15 purchase lines, country/rate selector, period (monthly or quarterly)
- Outputs: TVA return statement (déclaration), net TVA payable/refundable, SYSCOHADA journal entries for collection and payment, credit carry-forward

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** The journal-entry playground has a TVA transaction in its scenario library, but students cannot experiment with the aggregate return. This playground closes the gap between individual transaction entries and the monthly tax obligation.

### P-5 · Financial Ratio Analyzer
**EN:** Financial Ratio Analyzer
**FR:** Analyseur de Ratios Financiers

**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master, practicing financial analyst

**Pedagogical objective:** Students memorize ratio formulas for exams but rarely see all ratios computed simultaneously from the same balance sheet, nor do they understand how a single change (e.g., drawing down more long-term debt) propagates across liquidity, leverage, and activity ratios at once. SYSCOHADA's specific terminology (fonds de roulement, besoin en fonds de roulement, trésorerie nette) is also under-taught numerically.

**Core interaction:** Learner fills in a simplified SYSCOHADA bilan (actif immobilisé, actif circulant sub-categories, capitaux propres, dettes financières, dettes circulantes) and compte de résultat (chiffre d'affaires, résultat brut, résultat net). The playground instantly computes ~15 ratios with traffic-light benchmarks and a spider chart. Sliders let the learner adjust one balance sheet item and watch all ratios recalculate.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: ~12 balance sheet + income statement line items using SYSCOHADA account class labels
- Outputs: Liquidity (liquidité générale/réduite), profitability (ROE, ROA, marge nette), leverage (gearing, autonomie financière), activity (rotation stocks, DSO, DPO), SYSCOHADA-specific (FR, BFR, TN), spider chart, benchmark traffic lights

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** CVP focuses on unit economics; this playground focuses on the aggregate financial health picture. It is the natural capstone to the journal-entry → financial statements pathway and the most directly useful tool for a Licence 3 dissertation or Master thesis financial analysis chapter.
