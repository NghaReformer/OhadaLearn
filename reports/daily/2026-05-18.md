### PG-01 — SYSCOHADA Trial Balance & Closing Entries
**EN:** Trial Balance & Period Close  
**FR:** Balance de vérification & clôture de période  
**Target learner:** Licence 2, early Licence 3  
**Pedagogical objective:** Students confuse the trial balance (a pre-close mechanical check) with financial statements, and often misapply the closing sequence — temporary accounts → income summary → retained earnings. This is the most common source of errors before final statements.  
**Core interaction:** Learner is given a populated general ledger (pre-built or built in the Journal Entries playground via shared state). They must: (1) drag account balances to the correct side of a two-column trial balance, (2) verify it balances, (3) post the four closing entries in the correct order, (4) observe retained earnings updating on the balance sheet.  
**Minimum viable inputs:** Populated ledger (10–15 accounts), current period. **Outputs:** balanced trial balance, post-closing trial balance, updated equity section.  
**Complements existing playgrounds:** Natural continuation of the Journal Entries playground — same ledger state can be piped in via the `SharedResourceType` interface (`chart-of-accounts`). Fills the gap between posting entries and producing statements.

### PG-02 — Budget vs. Actual Variance Analysis
**EN:** Budget Variance Analysis  
**FR:** Analyse des écarts budgétaires  
**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master 1, practicing management accountant  
**Pedagogical objective:** Management accounting students struggle to decompose a total budget variance into its price and volume (efficiency) components, and to determine whether each is favorable or unfavorable. This is core SYSCOHADA managerial accounting content.  
**Core interaction:** Learner sets budgeted unit cost, budgeted volume, then inputs actual cost and actual volume. The playground decomposes the total variance into: (a) price variance = (actual price − budget price) × actual quantity, (b) volume variance = (actual quantity − budget quantity) × budget price. Each cell is color-coded F/U. A waterfall chart shows the decomposition visually.  
**Minimum viable inputs:** Product/cost center name, budgeted unit cost, budgeted volume, actual unit cost, actual volume. **Outputs:** price variance, volume variance, total variance, each labeled favorable/unfavorable.  
**Complements existing playgrounds:** CVP covers static break-even; this covers dynamic period-end control — a distinct managerial accounting skill rarely covered by existing tools.

### PG-03 — Accounts Receivable Aging & Bad Debt Provision
**EN:** Receivables Aging & Allowance  
**FR:** Balance âgée des créances & provision pour créances douteuses  
**Target learner:** Licence 2, Licence 3  
**Pedagogical objective:** Students rarely understand the aging method (méthode de ventilation par ancienneté) used in SYSCOHADA to compute the provision for doubtful accounts. The misconception is applying a flat percentage to total receivables instead of tiered rates by aging bucket.  
**Core interaction:** Learner enters a set of receivable balances and their age (0–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ days). The playground applies configurable provision rates to each bucket (e.g., 0%, 10%, 30%, 100%) and computes the total provision. Learner then posts the adjusting journal entry (Debit: Dotation aux provisions / Credit: Provision pour créances douteuses) using the journal-entry engine.  
**Minimum viable inputs:** 5–10 receivable balances with dates, provision rates per aging bucket. **Outputs:** aging schedule, provision amount per bucket, total provision, journal entry.  
**Complements existing playgrounds:** Bank Reconciliation handles the cash side; this handles the receivables side of working capital management. Both use the same SYSCOHADA chart of accounts.

### PG-04 — Inventory Valuation: FIFO vs. Weighted Average
**EN:** Inventory Valuation Methods  
**FR:** Méthodes d'évaluation des stocks (CMUP / FIFO)  
**Target learner:** Licence 2  
**Pedagogical objective:** SYSCOHADA mandates FIFO or weighted average cost (CMUP — coût moyen unitaire pondéré). Students confuse which method yields higher COGS under rising prices and cannot trace the inventory card (fiche de stock) entries.  
**Core interaction:** Learner records a sequence of purchases (date, quantity, unit cost) and sales (date, quantity). The playground shows two parallel inventory cards — one FIFO, one CMUP — updating in real time with each transaction. It computes ending inventory value and COGS under each method, with a summary comparing the two outcomes.  
**Minimum viable inputs:** 4–8 purchase/sale transactions with dates, quantities, and unit costs. **Outputs:** FIFO inventory card, CMUP inventory card, ending inventory value, COGS, difference between methods.  
**Complements existing playgrounds:** Amortization and Depreciation cover long-term assets; this covers current asset valuation — a distinct balance sheet section with different accounting rules.

### PG-05 — Foreign Exchange Transaction & Revaluation
**EN:** FX Transaction & Year-End Revaluation  
**FR:** Transaction en devises & réévaluation de fin d'exercice  
**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master 1  
**Pedagogical objective:** SYSCOHADA requires year-end revaluation of monetary items in foreign currency (Compte 476/477 — écarts de conversion). Students do not understand why the exchange gain/loss appears on the balance sheet (not the income statement) under SYSCOHADA, unlike IFRS.  
**Core interaction:** Learner enters a foreign-currency transaction (e.g., import payable in USD), the transaction-date rate, and the closing rate. The playground posts the original entry, then computes the revaluation adjustment and posts the écart de conversion entry (Compte 476 or 477). A comparison panel shows the SYSCOHADA vs. IFRS treatment side-by-side to highlight the rule difference.  
**Minimum viable inputs:** Transaction amount (FCY), transaction date, spot rate at date, closing rate. **Outputs:** original journal entry, revaluation entry, balance sheet impact (SYSCOHADA accounts 476/477), IFRS comparison entry.  
**Complements existing playgrounds:** The existing playgrounds use a single currency via the shared `currency` preference. This is the first to handle multi-currency transactions — a natural extension once learners master single-currency entries. Builds directly on the Journal Entries infrastructure.
