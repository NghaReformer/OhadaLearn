### B-1 · HIGH — Undefined CSS custom properties `--orange`, `--blue`, `--red` permanently fall back to hardcoded hex

**Files:**
- `src/lib/components/playground/KpiStrip.svelte:76`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/amortization/components/ScheduleTable.svelte:240,246,259,265`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/amortization/components/ChartPanel.svelte:436,444,448,493,501,505,515,529,533`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/ReconciliationStatement.svelte:275,279,292,293`

`var(--orange, #f59e0b)`, `var(--blue, #3b82f6)`, and `var(--red, #ef4444)` are used throughout amortization and bank-reconciliation components, but `css-generator.ts` never emits `--orange`, `--blue`, or `--red` — only `--amber`, `--accent`, and `--error`. The fallback hex is therefore always active, permanently bypassing the theme system. Any future theme change or dark-mode variant will not affect these chart colors.

**Fix:** In `css-generator.ts`, add:
```
--orange: ${c.amber};
--blue: ${c.accent};
--red: ${c.error};
```
…or replace usages site-wide with the canonical token names (`--amber`, `--accent`, `--error`).

### B-8 · LOW — `VarianceScale` mixes static and dynamic SVG transform via a single inline style

**File:** `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/VarianceScale.svelte:146`

`transform-origin` and `transform-box` are static and should live in the `<style>` block on `.beam-group`. Only `transform: rotate(...)` is dynamic. Keeping all three in inline style adds unnecessary specificity and is harder to override in tests or future theme work.

### B-9 · LOW — No font-size or spacing tokens in theme — 456 hardcoded `font-size:` values across components

**Scope:** Every `.svelte` file with scoped styles

`ThemeTokens` and `css-generator.ts` define no typographic scale or spacing scale — only colors, font families, radii, shadows, and transitions. This means 456 `font-size:` declarations across the codebase (e.g. `0.6875rem`, `0.8125rem`, `1.75rem`) are arbitrary per-file choices with no shared baseline. A design system change (e.g. base font from 16px → 14px) requires touching every file.

**Fix (medium-term):** Add a `typeScale` map to `ThemeTokens` (e.g. `xs: '0.75rem'`, `sm: '0.875rem'`, `base: '1rem'`, `lg: '1.125rem'`, `xl: '1.25rem'`, `2xl: '1.5rem'`) and emit `--text-xs` … `--text-2xl` from `css-generator.ts`. Migrate hardcoded sizes incrementally.

### P-1 — Accounts Receivable Aging & Provisioning
**EN:** Receivables Aging & Bad-Debt Provision  
**FR:** Vieillissement des créances & Dépréciation des créances douteuses

**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master 1, practicing accountants

**Pedagogical objective:** Students routinely miscalculate the doubtful-debts provision under SYSCOHADA (account 491 — Dépréciation des comptes clients) by confusing the aging bucket rate with the cumulative amount. This playground makes the cascade from invoice date → aging bucket → provision rate → journal entry concrete and reversible.

**Core interaction:** Learner inputs a list of customer invoices (date, amount, customer). The playground auto-buckets into 0–30 d / 31–60 d / 61–90 d / >90 d. Per-bucket provision rates are adjustable (OHADA defaults: 0%, 25%, 50%, 100%). A live summary shows total provision, the delta from last period, and the resulting adjusting journal entry (debit 6591, credit 491).

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: invoice table (date, amount), bucket thresholds, provision rates per bucket
- Outputs: aging matrix, total provision, journal entry (SYSCOHADA accounts)

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Bank Reconciliation covers the bank side of cash; this covers the receivables side. Journal Entry covers recording mechanics; this adds the analytical layer (aging logic → provision rate) that precedes the entry.

### P-2 — VAT Computation & Monthly Settlement
**EN:** VAT Calculator & Monthly Return  
**FR:** Calculateur TVA & Déclaration mensuelle

**Target learner:** Licence 2/3, practicing tax accountants

**Pedagogical objective:** OHADA-zone VAT rates differ by country (Cameroon 19.25%, Ivory Coast 18%, Senegal 18%, DRC 16%, etc.) and students conflate input tax recovery, exempt supplies, and the due/credit balance. This playground surfaces why the VAT account (445x) can be either an asset or a liability.

**Core interaction:** Two ledger panels — Purchases (TVA déductible, account 4456) and Sales (TVA collectée, account 4457). Learner inputs invoices on each side. The playground computes the net VAT payable or recoverable for the period, and generates the settlement journal entry (4457 vs 4456 vs 4455).

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: country selector (drives default rate), purchase invoice list, sale invoice list, exempt-supply toggles
- Outputs: input VAT total, output VAT total, net balance, journal entry, month-end declaration form preview

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry covers the recording step; this playground covers the analysis step (computing what to record) and adds the country-specific policy dimension absent elsewhere.

### P-3 — Working Capital Cycle (Besoin en Fonds de Roulement)
**EN:** Working Capital & Cash Cycle Analyzer  
**FR:** Analyse du BFR et du cycle de trésorerie

**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master 1

**Pedagogical objective:** The FRNG / BFR / TN (Fonds de Roulement Net Global / Besoin en Fonds de Roulement / Trésorerie Nette) triangle is a core SYSCOHADA financial analysis tool. Students consistently confuse a positive BFR (a financing need) with a positive sign in the cash balance. Showing the live cash implication of changing collection or payment policies removes this confusion.

**Core interaction:** Learner sets revenue, COGS, and three sliders: Days Sales Outstanding (DSO), Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO), Days Payable Outstanding (DPO). The playground computes BFR = (DSO + DIO − DPO) × daily sales, FRNG from a simplified balance sheet, and TN = FRNG − BFR. A cash-cycle timeline (Gantt-style) shows how changes in payment terms affect the gap between cash outflow and cash inflow.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: annual revenue, COGS, DSO / DIO / DPO sliders
- Outputs: BFR (FCFA/XOF), FRNG, TN, cash cycle timeline, sensitivity table (±10 days per driver)

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** CVP explains how profit is earned; this explains when and how cash becomes available — a distinct concept that students often merge.

### P-4 — Budgetary Control & Variance Dashboard
**EN:** Budget vs. Actual Variance Tracker  
**FR:** Tableau de Contrôle Budgétaire

**Target learner:** Master 1/2, public-sector and corporate finance professionals

**Pedagogical objective:** In OHADA-zone entities (especially government bodies and NGOs), budget adherence is a legal requirement. Students and practitioners mix up the sign convention for favorable vs. unfavorable variances and fail to isolate volume variances from price variances. This playground builds that muscle by requiring the learner to classify each variance before revealing the answer.

**Core interaction:** Learner inputs a 12-month budget line-by-line (by SYSCOHADA account class). Actuals are input month by month. The dashboard computes period variance, cumulative variance, and percentage consumed, with traffic-light severity (≤5% yellow, >5% red). A goal-seek mode lets the learner work backward: "what actual spend in December keeps us under 3% cumulative variance?"

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: account list with annual budget, monthly actuals (entered progressively)
- Outputs: variance table (period + cumulative), waterfall chart, favorable/unfavorable classification, goal-seek panel

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** CVP asks "what volume is needed?"; budgetary control asks "how well are we tracking against plan?" — the temporal, multi-period dimension is entirely new.

### P-5 — Standard Costing & Variance Analysis
**EN:** Standard Cost & Variance Analyzer  
**FR:** Coûts Préétablis & Analyse des Écarts

**Target learner:** Master 1, industrial-accounting students

**Pedagogical objective:** Standard costing appears in every OHADA industrial-accounting exam, yet students routinely mis-sign the price/quantity/efficiency split. The common misconception: a positive quantity variance always means you used more material. This playground makes the decomposition visual and interactive.

**Core interaction:** Learner sets standard cost (price per unit × standard quantity). Then inputs actual production volume, actual unit price, and actual quantity used. The playground decomposes the total cost variance into Price Variance (SP vs AP × AQ) and Quantity/Efficiency Variance (SQ vs AQ × SP), with a bar chart waterfall. A "flip the assumption" toggle lets the learner see how the split changes under the alternative decomposition order.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: standard price, standard quantity per unit, actual price, actual quantity used, actual output
- Outputs: total variance, price variance, quantity variance (with sign and F/U label), variance waterfall chart, journal entry for booking the variances (accounts 6xx vs 39x under SYSCOHADA)

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** CVP works with aggregate cost behavior; standard costing works with per-unit cost decomposition. The two are often taught back-to-back in SYSCOHADA curricula but address different analytical frames — this closes the gap.
