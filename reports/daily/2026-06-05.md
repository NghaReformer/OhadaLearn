### High

#### H-1 — Hardcoded hex colors in JS data objects (not CSS fallbacks)

**Severity:** High  
**Files:**
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/CategoryBreakdownDonut.svelte:30–39`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/MatchingPairsOverlay.svelte:30–34`

`CATEGORY_COLORS` and `COLOR` are plain TypeScript objects whose values are raw hex strings (`'#6ea8fe'`, `'#22c55e'`, `'#f59e0b'`, etc.). These are passed directly into SVG `fill`/`stroke` attributes and `style="background: {seg.color}"`, completely bypassing the theme token system.  
This means a theme change in `tokens.ts` will **not** update these visuals, and contrast ratios are unverified against the dark theme.

**Suggested fix:** Replace each hard-coded hex with a `getComputedStyle(document.documentElement).getPropertyValue('--accent')` lookup inside an `$effect`, or define a typed mapping from category → CSS custom property name and apply it via a CSS class (e.g., `class="cat-outstanding-check"`), letting the stylesheet do the coloring via `var(--accent)` etc.

#### M-3 — Legacy `tweened` stores mix Svelte 4 store pattern into Svelte 5 codebase

**Severity:** Medium  
**Files:**
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/ReconciliationFlow.svelte:96` — `const meetTween = tweened(0, …); … $meetTween`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/VarianceScale.svelte:50–52` — three `tweened` stores
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/CategoryBreakdownDonut.svelte:65` — `const totalTween = tweened(…)`

`tweened` from `svelte/motion` returns a Svelte 4 writable store. Accessing it in templates via `$meetTween` uses the legacy auto-subscription protocol. This works via Svelte 5's compatibility layer, but contradicts CLAUDE.md's mandate to use rune-based state and creates an inconsistency with how every other animation/state is managed in the project.

**Suggested fix:** Replace each `tweened` with a `$state` + `$effect` pattern using Svelte 5's native `untrack`/timer approach, or use the `@motion` package if available, keeping the API consistent with runes.

### P-1 — TVA / VAT Computation Playground

**Title (EN):** VAT Settlement Calculator  
**Title (FR):** Calculateur de liquidation de TVA  
**Target learner:** Licence 2, or practicing accountant/tax manager  
**Pedagogical objective:** Students in the OHADA zone routinely confuse _TVA collectée_, _TVA déductible_, and the prorata for partially-exempt entities. This playground forces the learner to construct the monthly/quarterly VAT return step by step.  
**Core interaction:** Input a list of sales invoices (with applicable TVA rate: 19.25 % Cameroon, 18 % Côte d'Ivoire, etc.) and purchase invoices. The learner must classify each line, apply the correct deductibility rule, and compute the net tax due or credit to carry forward. Visual T-account shows the two liability sides balancing.  
**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:** Locale (currency + country VAT rate), list of N sales lines (HT amount, taxable yes/no), list of M purchase lines (HT amount, deductibility rule). Outputs: TVA collectée total, TVA déductible total, solde net, journal entry for the settlement.  
**Complements existing:** Journal Entry playground handles the resulting accounting entry; this playground teaches the tax _calculation_ that precedes it. Together they cover the full tax → accounting workflow.

### P-2 — Trial Balance & Year-End Adjustments

**Title (EN):** Trial Balance & Adjusting Entries  
**Title (FR):** Balance de vérification et écritures de régularisation  
**Target learner:** Licence 2 / Licence 3  
**Pedagogical objective:** Learners who can post individual journal entries (covered by the Journal Entry playground) still struggle with the _period-end_ workflow: producing an unadjusted trial balance, identifying deferral/accrual items (charges à payer, produits à recevoir, charges constatées d'avance), and understanding how adjustments flow into the adjusted trial balance.  
**Core interaction:** A pre-loaded set of 10–15 account balances is displayed as an unadjusted trial balance. A sidebar lists 4–6 adjustment scenarios (prepaid insurance, accrued salaries, deferred revenue, depreciation to record). The learner drags scenarios onto the trial balance, enters the amounts, and watches the adjusted totals update. Incorrect adjustments trigger targeted feedback explaining the double-entry impact.  
**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:** Unadjusted account balances, 4–6 scenario cards (each with type, amount, description). Outputs: adjusted trial balance, list of adjusting entries generated.  
**Complements existing:** Extends Journal Entry by introducing period-end close context; acts as a natural bridge to financial statements.

### P-3 — Inventory Valuation Methods

**Title (EN):** Inventory Cost Flow — FIFO vs. Weighted Average  
**Title (FR):** Méthodes d'évaluation des stocks — CMUP vs FIFO  
**Target learner:** Licence 2  
**Pedagogical objective:** SYSCOHADA mandates _coût moyen unitaire pondéré_ (CMUP/weighted average) for inventory, but many students cannot articulate why the method choice affects COGS and closing stock, especially when prices fluctuate. The misconception "FIFO and CMUP give the same result" is widespread.  
**Core interaction:** The learner enters a sequence of 6–10 purchase and sale events (date, qty, unit price). Side-by-side panels show the stock ledger under FIFO and CMUP simultaneously updating after each event. A summary strip shows the difference in COGS and ending inventory under each method, with the SYSCOHADA-mandated method highlighted.  
**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:** Sequence of buy/sell events (date, type, qty, price). Outputs: stock ledger table per method, COGS and ending stock comparison, delta impact on gross profit.  
**Complements existing:** None of the existing playgrounds touches inventory. This fills a major SYSCOHADA curriculum gap (Classes 3/6 accounts).

### P-4 — Payroll Accounting (Fiche de paie OHADA)

**Title (EN):** Payroll Computation & Journal Entry  
**Title (FR):** Bulletin de paie et comptabilisation des charges de personnel  
**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master, or HR/accounting professional  
**Pedagogical objective:** Payroll is one of the most error-prone monthly tasks in OHADA-zone companies. Students cannot distinguish employer charges (CNPS, CAMU, etc.) from employee deductions (IRPP, CNPS worker share), and the resulting multi-line journal entry (debit 661, credit 42x/43x/44x) is intimidating.  
**Core interaction:** The learner inputs gross salary, allowances (logement, transport), and the applicable country (Cameroon, Côte d'Ivoire, Sénégal — with different CNPS and tax brackets). The playground computes the full pay slip step by step (brut → cotisations sociales → brut imposable → IRPP → net à payer), then generates the complete SYSCOHADA journal entry with account codes from the chart of accounts system already in the app.  
**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:** Country, gross salary, allowances, number of dependents (for IRPP). Outputs: pay slip breakdown, employer cost total, net salary, journal entry.  
**Complements existing:** Uses the existing Chart of Accounts browser (Classes 4/6), the format/currency system (XAF default), and i18n. No other playground touches personnel costs.

### P-5 — Statement of Cash Flows (Indirect Method)

**Title (EN):** Cash Flow Statement Builder  
**Title (FR):** Tableau des flux de trésorerie (méthode indirecte)  
**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master  
**Pedagogical objective:** The indirect method reconciliation is the most-failed topic in advanced SYSCOHADA courses. Students understand the individual adjustments in isolation (add back depreciation, subtract increase in receivables) but cannot sequence them correctly or explain why operating cash flow diverges from net income.  
**Core interaction:** The learner is given a simplified income statement and two-year comparative balance sheet. They drag adjustment cards (depreciation, change in AR, change in AP, change in inventory, gain/loss on asset disposal) into the three cash-flow sections (operating/investing/financing). Each dropped card updates a running total. A visual bridge diagram shows the path from net income to net cash from operations in real time.  
**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:** Net income, depreciation, changes in six working-capital line items, investing transactions (asset sale/purchase), financing (debt repayment, dividends). Outputs: fully classified cash flow statement, reconciliation check (± zero).  
**Complements existing:** Closes the financial-reporting loop opened by the Trial Balance playground (P-2); together the two cover the full cycle from adjusting entries to published financial statements.
