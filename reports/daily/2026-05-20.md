**Files (30+ instances):**

- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/ScenarioWalkthrough.svelte:234,274`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/VarianceScale.svelte:230,260–261,331,335,370,416`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/ReconciliationFlow.svelte:278`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/BankStatementPanel.svelte:175`

**Explanation:** `css-generator.ts` emits `--amber` (from `tokens.ts`), `--green`, `--error`, and `--accent` — but **never** `--orange`, `--blue`, or `--red`. Every `var(--orange, #f59e0b)` / `var(--blue, #3b82f6)` / `var(--red, #ef4444)` silently falls through to its hardcoded fallback, bypassing the theme system entirely. A theme change or token rename has zero effect on these colours.

**Suggested fix:** Add `--orange: ${c.amber};` (or a new `orange` token), `--blue: <new token>;`, and `--red: ${c.error};` to `css-generator.ts`, and add matching entries to `ThemeTokens` in `tokens.ts`. Alternatively, migrate all usages to the already-defined `--amber`, `--error`, and a new `--blue` token.

### Idea 1 — Inventory Valuation Playground
**EN:** Inventory Costing Methods | **FR:** Méthodes d'évaluation des stocks

**Target learner:** Licence 2 (intermediate financial accounting)

**Pedagogical objective:** Students conflate FIFO, LIFO, and Weighted-Average Cost (CMP) — the three methods produce dramatically different COGS and ending-inventory figures, and SYSCOHADA mandates CMP or FIFO (LIFO is prohibited). The playground makes the divergence visceral through a shared set of purchase lots.

**Core interaction:** Learner enters 3–6 purchase transactions (date, qty, unit cost) and 2–4 sales (date, qty). Tabs switch between FIFO, CMP perpetual, and CMP periodic. A live results table shows: running stock layers → COGS per sale → ending inventory value → gross profit. A bar chart compares gross profit across all three methods side-by-side for the same data.

**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:**
- Inputs: purchase rows (date, qty, price), sale rows (date, qty), opening stock
- Outputs: method-specific stock ledger, total COGS, ending inventory, gross profit

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry shows the mechanics of recording; this playground teaches *what value* to record, directly feeding the SYSCOHADA Class 3 (Stocks) account entries that students then practice in Journal Entry.

### Idea 2 — Financial Ratio Dashboard
**EN:** Financial Ratio Analyser | **FR:** Analyseur de ratios financiers

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master 1 (financial analysis)

**Pedagogical objective:** Students can calculate ratios mechanically but cannot read a balance sheet holistically. This playground forces them to interpret a complete SYSCOHADA-format balance sheet and income statement simultaneously, seeing how one input change cascades across all ratios.

**Core interaction:** A two-panel layout: left side is a simplified SYSCOHADA balance sheet + income statement with editable cells (pre-filled with a realistic Cameroonian SME scenario). Right side is a live ratio dashboard grouped into four SYSCOHADA-standard families — Liquidity, Solvency, Profitability, and Activity. Each ratio tile shows the computed value, the SYSCOHADA benchmark range, and a traffic-light indicator. Changing any cell in the left panel instantly updates all ratio tiles.

**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:**
- Inputs: ~15 balance sheet line items, ~8 income statement line items
- Outputs: current ratio, quick ratio, debt/equity, ROA, ROE, asset turnover, DSO, DPO + benchmark indicators

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** CVP covers internal cost structure; this playground covers external solvency and profitability reporting — the two complementary lenses of SYSCOHADA management control.

### Idea 3 — VAT Invoice Builder
**EN:** VAT Invoice & Tax Computation | **FR:** Facture TVA et calcul de taxe

**Target learner:** Licence 1 (introductory) / practicing professionals

**Pedagogical objective:** Many students cannot correctly distinguish HT (hors taxe), TVA collectée, and TVA déductible, and conflate the VAT journal entry with the invoice total. The OHADA zone has multiple VAT rates (19.25 % in Cameroon, 18 % in Côte d'Ivoire, etc.) that students frequently mix up.

**Core interaction:** Learner builds an invoice line-by-line: description, qty, unit price HT, VAT rate (dropdown with country presets). A live invoice preview shows HT total, TVA, TTC. Below, an accounting section shows the required SYSCOHADA journal entries (Class 4 client account, Class 7 revenue, Class 4435 TVA collectée). A "reverse" toggle switches to a supplier invoice to show TVA déductible (Class 4456) instead.

**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:**
- Inputs: invoice lines, VAT rate (country preset or custom %), invoice direction (sale/purchase)
- Outputs: rendered invoice total, journal entries with account codes (SYSCOHADA chart)

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry requires the learner to already know what to write; this playground derives the entries automatically from a business document, teaching the document-to-entry mapping that is the hardest conceptual step in Licence 1.

### Idea 4 — Budget Variance Analyser
**EN:** Budget vs. Actual Variance Analysis | **FR:** Analyse des écarts budget / réalisé

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master (management accounting / contrôle de gestion)

**Pedagogical objective:** Students confuse price variance, volume variance, and mix variance. SYSCOHADA's contrôle de gestion curriculum requires students to decompose total variance into these components and correctly label them favorable (F) or unfavorable (U) — a task with many sign-error traps.

**Core interaction:** Learner enters budget and actual figures for up to three cost centres: fixed costs, variable cost/unit, and production volume. The playground computes and decomposes: total variance → price variance (rate effect) → volume variance (quantity effect). Colour-coded cells (green = F, red = U) and a waterfall chart show how each component contributes to the total deviation. A goal-seek panel lets the learner reverse-engineer: "what actual volume would have given zero variance?"

**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:**
- Inputs: budget unit cost, budget volume, actual unit cost, actual volume (per cost centre)
- Outputs: price variance, volume variance, total variance, F/U labels, waterfall chart

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** CVP shows break-even; this playground extends into the post-period analysis phase — what actually happened versus what was planned. Together they cover the full SYSCOHADA management-accounting cycle.

### Idea 5 — Payroll & CNPS Calculator
**EN:** Payroll & Social Charges Calculator | **FR:** Simulateur de bulletin de paie et charges CNPS

**Target learner:** Licence 2–3 / practicing HR and accounting professionals

**Pedagogical objective:** Payroll is the most feared practical exercise in SYSCOHADA exams because of the multiple CNPS rates (employee 2.8 %, employer 13.35–17.45 % in Cameroon), the IRPP progressive brackets, the withholding threshold rules, and the Class 4 / Class 6 / Class 4 journal entry chain. Students routinely mis-map charges between "charge de personnel" (Class 66) and "charge patronales" (Class 65/4).

**Core interaction:** Learner enters gross salary, benefit-in-kind values, seniority, and number of dependents. The playground computes: CNPS salarié, CNPS patronal, IRPP (progressive brackets displayed as a stepped bar), net à payer. Below, the full journal entry set is auto-generated: debit 661 (salaires bruts), credit 421 (personnel à payer), credit 4331 (CNPS salarié), debit 664 (charges patronales), credit 4332 (CNPS patronal). Changing any input live-updates all values.

**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:**
- Inputs: gross salary, benefits, dependents, country preset (Cameroon / Côte d'Ivoire / Sénégal)
- Outputs: pay-slip breakdown, net salary, employer cost, journal entries with SYSCOHADA codes

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** No existing playground touches Class 4 (personnel) or Class 6 (charges) accounts. This fills the most practically-demanded gap in the SYSCOHADA curriculum and is the most-requested topic by African accounting students in professional forums.
