### BUG-01 · **Critical** · `src/routes/[lang]/playgrounds/[slug]/+page.svelte:103`

**`{#await}` block missing `{:catch}` handler — silent failure on playground load error**

The `{#await nativeLoader}` block that loads native Svelte playground components has a `{:then module}` branch but no `{:catch error}` branch. If the dynamic import fails (network error, chunk not found, Vite build issue), the user sees a permanently blank panel with no error message and no recovery path.

**Fix:** Add `{:catch err}` with an error card using `var(--error)` and the existing `common.error.generic` i18n key, and log `err` to console so it surfaces in monitoring.

### BUG-03 · **High** · `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/CategoryBreakdownDonut.svelte:31–39`

**9 hardcoded hex colors in a JavaScript object — completely outside the theme system**

```ts
const CATEGORY_COLORS: Record<ItemCategory, string> = {
    'outstanding-check': '#6ea8fe',
    'deposit-in-transit': '#22c55e',
    'bank-charge': '#f59e0b',
    // …6 more
};
```

These colors are passed directly to SVG `fill` attributes via `style="background: {seg.color}"`. They cannot be overridden by theme changes, will not respect a future light-mode, and several duplicates of `--accent`, `--green`, `--amber`, and `--error` values.

**Fix:** Replace with references to CSS custom properties read via `getComputedStyle(document.documentElement)` at mount time, or restructure to assign CSS classes per category and use `fill: currentColor` or CSS `var()` in scoped styles.

### IDEA-01 — Inventory Valuation Methods

**EN:** Inventory Valuation Methods  
**FR:** Méthodes d'évaluation des stocks

**Target learner:** Licence 2 / Licence 3

**Pedagogical objective:** SYSCOHADA's *Plan comptable* (class 3) uses weighted average cost (CMUP) as the primary inventory method; IFRS also permits FIFO. Students routinely confuse the methods and cannot explain why profit differs between them under price inflation or deflation. This playground fixes that by making the comparison visceral.

**Core interaction:** The learner enters 3–6 purchase lots with quantities and unit prices, then records a series of sales. A side-by-side table shows CMUP (after each lot or periodic), FIFO, and optionally LIFO closing stock values, cost of goods sold, and gross margin. A toggle switches between "moving CMUP" (SYSCOHADA default) and "periodic CMUP". Live chart shows how gross margin diverges between methods as price trend changes.

**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:**  
Inputs: purchase lots (qty, unit cost), sales lots (qty), period (to compute periodic CMUP)  
Outputs: closing stock value, COGS, gross margin per method; journal entries for stock movements (class 3 ↔ 6)

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry covers the double-entry mechanics; this teaches *what value goes in* those entries — the upstream decision the JE playground assumes as given.

### IDEA-02 — Financial Ratio Dashboard

**EN:** Financial Ratio Analyzer  
**FR:** Analyseur de ratios financiers

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master 1 / practicing professional

**Pedagogical objective:** Students can recite ratio formulas but cannot diagnose a firm from a real balance sheet + income statement. This playground teaches interpretation: is a current ratio of 1.2 good or dangerous? Depends on industry and receivables quality.

**Core interaction:** Learner enters simplified SYSCOHADA-format balance sheet and income statement line items (assets by class 2/3/4/5, liabilities by class 1/4/5, revenue, charges by nature). The playground computes a panel of ratios grouped by category — liquidity (FR: *liquidité*), solvency (*solvabilité*), profitability (*rentabilité*), activity (*rotation*) — and colour-codes each against sector benchmarks selectable from a dropdown (commerce, manufacturing, services, agriculture — OHADA zone norms). A radar chart shows the overall financial profile.

**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:**  
Inputs: 15–20 financial statement line items, sector selection  
Outputs: 12–15 computed ratios, colour-coded assessment, radar chart, narrative diagnostic text (i18n)

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Downstream from Journal Entry + Bank Reconciliation — the learner finally sees what all those entries add up to.

### IDEA-03 — Budget Variance Analysis

**EN:** Budget Variance Analyzer  
**FR:** Analyseur d'écarts budgétaires

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master 1 / management accountant

**Pedagogical objective:** The gap between budget and actual figures is a core managerial accounting skill (SYSCOHADA compta analytique, section 9). Students conflate "favourable variance" with "good" and "adverse variance" with "bad" — which breaks when a favourable volume variance hides a degraded margin mix. This playground builds intuition for decomposing variances.

**Core interaction:** Learner enters budget and actual figures for volume sold, selling price, variable cost per unit, and fixed costs. The playground decomposes total profit variance into: volume variance, price variance, variable-cost rate variance, and fixed-cost spending variance. Each is shown with a waterfall bar chart. A "what-if" mode lets the learner isolate the effect of changing one driver while holding others constant.

**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:**  
Inputs: budget qty, budget price, budget VC/unit, budget FC; actual qty, actual price, actual VC/unit, actual FC  
Outputs: 4 variance components, total variance, waterfall chart, favourable/adverse flags, management commentary templates (i18n)

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Connects directly to CVP (the playground that sets up the cost structure) and adds the temporal, managerial-control dimension that CVP's static break-even analysis lacks.

### IDEA-04 — Payroll & Social Charges Calculator

**EN:** Payroll & Social Charges  
**FR:** Calcul de la paie et des charges sociales

**Target learner:** Licence 2 / practicing bookkeeper / HR administrator

**Pedagogical objective:** Payroll is one of the highest-frequency bookkeeping tasks in OHADA-zone SMEs, yet most accounting courses gloss over the detail. Students do not know how to split gross salary into CNPS (caisse nationale de prévoyance sociale) employee vs employer shares, income tax (IRPP) brackets, or the resulting journal entries (class 4 accounts: 421, 431, 437, 447). Rates vary by country; the playground exposes this explicitly.

**Core interaction:** Learner selects OHADA-zone country (Cameroon, Côte d'Ivoire, Sénégal, default generic), enters gross salary, optional benefits in kind, and number of dependants. The playground computes net salary, employee deductions (CNPS + IRPP), employer charges (CNPS + FDFP/FNPH), and total wage cost. A breakdown table shows each line item. A "journal entry" panel shows the complete payroll entry and the payment entry.

**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:**  
Inputs: country, gross salary, benefits in kind, dependants  
Outputs: employee deductions itemised, employer charges itemised, net pay, total labour cost, two journal entries (salary booking + payment), payslip summary

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry covers mechanics; this provides a real, high-frequency business context that auto-fills realistic debit/credit amounts, bridging abstract accounting rules to the HR workflow every SME performs monthly.

### IDEA-05 — Fixed Asset Disposal & Revaluation

**EN:** Fixed Asset Lifecycle  
**FR:** Cycle de vie d'une immobilisation

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master 1

**Pedagogical objective:** Asset disposal is the most frequently mis-journalised event in SYSCOHADA practical exams. Students struggle with three scenarios: disposal at book value (no gain/loss), disposal above book value (profit on disposal → class 77), and disposal below book value (loss → class 67). Revaluation (a SYSCOHADA-specific process for inflation adjustment) adds a fourth complexity. The Depreciation playground covers straight-line and declining balance; this playground picks up where it stops — at the end of life.

**Core interaction:** Learner enters asset cost, acquisition date, depreciation method and rate (carried over from Depreciation playground's output), and disposal date + proceeds. The playground computes book value at disposal date, gain/loss, and generates the full disposal journal entry (debit class 2 accumulated depreciation + disposal account, credit asset cost, then gain/loss). A separate "Revaluation" tab applies an index to restate the asset value and shows the resulting entry (class 1 — equity reserve).

**Minimum viable inputs/outputs:**  
Inputs: acquisition cost, date, depreciation method, rate, disposal date, proceeds  
Outputs: cumulative depreciation to disposal date, book value, gain/loss, disposal journal entry (4 lines), optional revaluation entry; all referencing SYSCOHADA class 2/7/6 accounts

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Closes the asset lifecycle loop that Depreciation opens. Together they give the learner end-to-end coverage of a fixed asset from purchase through depreciation through disposal — the three canonical exam questions in SYSCOHADA financial accounting.
