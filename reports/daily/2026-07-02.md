### Idea 1 — Trial Balance & Period Closing
**EN:** Trial Balance & Closing Entries | **FR:** Balance de vérification et clôture d'exercice

**Target learner:** Licence 1–2

**Pedagogical objective:** Students treat journal entries as isolated events; they don't see how entries accumulate into the trial balance and how period-end closing zeroes temporary accounts to produce the opening balance for the next period. This is the single most common conceptual gap in first-year financial accounting.

**Core interaction:** Post 6–10 guided journal entries for a SYSCOHADA micro-enterprise scenario. A live two-column trial balance updates with every entry. A "Run Closing" button generates the closing entries for classes 6 and 7, zeroes them into the income summary account, and reveals the post-closing balance.

**Minimum viable inputs:** Chart-of-accounts picker (reusing `src/lib/shared/chart-of-accounts`), debit/credit amounts for each entry, "Post" button. **Outputs:** Pre-closing trial balance, closing journal entries, post-closing balance, net income figure.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry teaches single-entry mechanics; this playground closes the loop to the full accounting cycle. Directly prerequisite to financial-statement construction.

### Idea 2 — Cash Flow Statement Builder
**EN:** Cash Flow Statement | **FR:** Tableau des flux de trésorerie (TFT)

**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master, practicing professional

**Pedagogical objective:** Students confuse activity classifications (operating vs. investing vs. financing) and can't reconcile the TFT's closing cash balance to the balance sheet. SYSCOHADA's indirect method format differs subtly from IFRS and French PCG.

**Core interaction:** Given a pre-populated set of balance-sheet changes and income statement items, the learner drags each line into the correct TFT section. Incorrect placements are flagged with an explanation. Once complete, the three-section total reconciles to the net change in cash shown on the balance sheet.

**Minimum viable inputs:** ~12 pre-set line items (depreciation add-back, inventory change, capex, loan proceeds, dividends paid, etc.); toggle between SYSCOHADA and IFRS layouts. **Outputs:** Completed TFT, pass/fail per section, final reconciliation check.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Sits downstream of Journal Entry and Bank Reconciliation; requires students to read the balance sheet and income statement outputs of those exercises.

### Idea 3 — Payroll & Social Charges (OHADA zone)
**EN:** Payroll & Social Charges | **FR:** Paie et charges sociales SYSCOHADA

**Target learner:** Licence 2–3, practicing professional

**Pedagogical objective:** OHADA-zone payroll involves multiple parallel deductions (CNPS employee + employer shares, IRPP withholding, AGA, transport cap rules) that interact in ways no standard textbook illustrates step-by-step. Practitioners and students routinely miscalculate net salary or misclassify the payroll journal entry.

**Core interaction:** Enter gross salary, allowances (transport, housing), family dependants, and select country (Cameroon/Côte d'Ivoire/Senegal) to apply the correct rates. A stepped computation panel reveals each deduction in sequence. The final output is a payslip preview + a SYSCOHADA class-6 journal entry.

**Minimum viable inputs:** Gross salary, allowances, country selector, number of dependants. **Outputs:** Gross → net waterfall, employer cost total, class 641/645 journal entry, CNPS/IRPP amounts with regulatory references.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Fills a domain gap absent from every existing playground; directly relevant to the practicing-professional segment.

### Idea 4 — VAT Return Simulator
**EN:** VAT Return | **FR:** Déclaration de TVA

**Target learner:** Licence 2, practicing professional

**Pedagogical objective:** Students understand VAT conceptually but fail when netting input against output VAT, handling credit carry-forwards across months, and producing the statutory declaration format used in OHADA-zone countries (TVA at 18–19.25 % depending on jurisdiction).

**Core interaction:** Enter purchase invoices (input VAT deductible) and sales invoices (output VAT collected) for a one-month period. The playground computes net VAT due or credit. A second-month panel lets students carry the credit forward, making the multi-period logic tangible.

**Minimum viable inputs:** 3–5 purchase invoices (amount + rate), 3–5 sales invoices, current credit brought forward. **Outputs:** Input VAT total, output VAT total, net position, monthly declaration table, journal entry for VAT payment/receivable.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry teaches individual transaction recording; this playground chains multiple entries into a compliance output and introduces regulatory time-boxing.

### Idea 5 — Financial Ratios Analyser
**EN:** Financial Ratios | **FR:** Analyse financière par ratios

**Target learner:** Licence 2–3, Master

**Pedagogical objective:** Students can compute individual ratios in isolation but can't interpret a ratio profile holistically, compare against sector benchmarks, or explain what a deteriorating current ratio signals about working-capital management. SYSCOHADA's balance-sheet format (permanent vs. circulating capital) maps differently to ratio formulas than IFRS formats.

**Core interaction:** Input a simplified balance sheet (5–8 line items) and income statement (3–4 line items) for a chosen SYSCOHADA scenario. Eight ratios compute live in four groups (liquidity, solvency, profitability, efficiency). Each ratio card shows the formula, the computed value, a sector benchmark range, and a one-sentence plain-language interpretation generated from thresholds.

**Minimum viable inputs:** Total assets, current assets, stocks, receivables, current liabilities, equity, revenue, operating profit, net profit. **Outputs:** Ratio dashboard with red/amber/green band indicators, written interpretation per ratio, exportable analysis summary.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Sits at the top of the accounting cycle — it consumes the outputs (financial statements) that every other playground helps produce. Natural capstone for Licence 3 or a firm's annual review.
