### 1.1 `--orange` token does not exist — 20 call-sites silently fall back to `#f59e0b` (high)

`src/lib/theme/tokens.ts` defines no `--orange` property; the theme uses `--amber` / `--amber-glow` instead. Every use of `var(--orange, #f59e0b)` silently renders the hardcoded hex colour, breaking the theme contract.

Affected files (non-exhaustive):

- `src/lib/components/playground/KpiStrip.svelte:76`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/amortization/components/ScheduleTable.svelte:240`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/amortization/components/ChartPanel.svelte:436, 493, 515, 529`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/LedgerPanel.svelte:175`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/VarianceScale.svelte:230, 260, 261`

**Suggested fix:** Either add `orange` to `ThemeTokens.colors` in `tokens.ts` (mapping to `#f59e0b`), or replace every `var(--orange, …)` with `var(--amber)`. The amber token (`#f5a623`) is already defined and semantically equivalent. Prefer `--amber` since the token already exists.

### 1.3 `$lib/server/` type imported into client component (medium)

**`src/lib/components/feedback/FeedbackForm.svelte:7`**

```ts
import type { FeedbackType, FeedbackSeverity } from '$lib/server/db/types';
```

`FeedbackType` and `FeedbackSeverity` are simple string-union types (`'bug' | 'feature' | 'other'` and `'low' | 'medium' | 'high' | 'blocker'`). Although `import type` is erased at runtime, SvelteKit's Vite plugin enforces the `$lib/server/` boundary at the module graph level and may emit a build warning or error in certain modes. The bigger concern is design: shared types used in client components should not live behind the server boundary.

**Suggested fix:** Move `FeedbackType` and `FeedbackSeverity` to a new file such as `src/lib/types/feedback.ts` (or co-locate them in a non-server location). Update `$lib/server/db/types.ts` to re-import from there if needed.

### 1.4 `{#await nativeLoader}` block missing `{:catch}` (medium)

**`src/routes/[lang]/playgrounds/[slug]/+page.svelte:103–116`**

```svelte
{#await nativeLoader}
  <div class="iframe-loading">…spinner…</div>
{:then module}
  {#if module}…{/if}
{/await}
```

No `{:catch}` block. If the dynamic import throws (network failure, malformed module, missing export), Svelte propagates the rejection to the nearest `<svelte:boundary>` or page-level error boundary. There is a `+error.svelte` at the `[lang]` level, but it will replace the entire page layout rather than showing an inline error in the playground container. Users navigating to a broken playground slug see the page-level error page instead of an in-context message.

**Suggested fix:**

```svelte
{:catch err}
  <div class="iframe-error" role="alert">
    <p>{$t('playgrounds.load_error')}</p>
    <pre class="error-detail">{err?.message}</pre>
  </div>
```

Add the i18n key `playgrounds.load_error` to both `playgrounds.en.ts` and `playgrounds.fr.ts`.

### 2.1 Financial Ratio Analysis — Analyse des ratios financiers

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master 1

**Pedagogical objective:** Students routinely compute ratios mechanically without interpreting what they signal or how to act on them. This playground builds the missing bridge between formula and decision: the learner sees a SYSCOHADA-formatted balance sheet and income statement, computes liquidity, solvency, profitability, and activity ratios, and reads colour-coded traffic-light feedback against OHADA-sector benchmarks.

**Core interaction:** The learner is given a real-looking company scenario (e.g. a West African import-export SME). They enter or adjust line items; the playground recomputes all 15+ ratios live and flags which fall outside healthy ranges with an explanation of the implication (e.g. "current ratio 0.8 — short-term obligations exceed liquid assets").

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: balance sheet (fixed assets, current assets, equity, long-term debt, current liabilities) + income statement (revenue, COGS, EBIT, net income)
- Outputs: liquidity ratios (current, quick, cash), solvency ratios (D/E, interest cover), profitability (ROE, ROA, net margin), activity (asset turnover, DSO, DIO)

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** The Journal Entry playground produces raw accounting data; this playground teaches what to do with it analytically. Pairs naturally with TVM for capital-structure decisions.

### 2.2 VAT / TVA Returns — Calculateur de déclaration TVA OHADA

**Target learner:** Licence 2 / practicing professional

**Pedagogical objective:** OHADA-zone VAT (TVA) varies significantly between member states in rate, exemption scope, and return frequency. Students confuse "TVA collectée" with "TVA déductible" and make systematic sign errors on net balances. They also do not know how to handle mixed-rate inputs or VAT credits.

**Core interaction:** The learner builds a list of purchase and sales invoices for a period, each tagged with a TVA rate (0%, 8.5%, 18%, or exempt — configurable per country: Cameroon, CI, Sénégal). The playground computes TVA collectée vs TVA déductible, the net balance (payable or credit carried forward), and generates the corresponding SYSCOHADA journal entry (account 443 / 445).

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: list of transactions (type: purchase/sale, amount HT, TVA rate, tax-exempt flag) + country selector
- Outputs: TVA collectée, TVA déductible, net TVA, draft return table, SYSCOHADA journal entry

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Bank Reconciliation and Journal Entry handle the recording side; TVA brings the tax compliance dimension that is mandatory for any practicing accountant in the OHADA zone.

### 2.3 Inventory Valuation Methods — Méthodes de valorisation des stocks

**Target learner:** Licence 2

**Pedagogical objective:** SYSCOHADA permits FIFO and Coût Moyen Pondéré (CMP), with CMP offered in two variants: weighted average at each entry (CUMP à chaque entrée) and end-of-period weighted average (CUMP de fin de période). Students consistently confuse these and under-appreciate how the choice affects COGS and closing inventory value, impacting profit.

**Core interaction:** The learner enters a sequence of stock movements (date, type: entry/exit, quantity, unit cost for entries). The playground shows three side-by-side schedules — FIFO, CUMP à chaque entrée, CUMP de fin de période — updating in real time. A summary strip shows the difference in closing stock value and COGS across methods, surfacing the accounting impact of the choice.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: opening stock (qty, unit cost) + list of movements (date, in/out, qty, unit cost)
- Outputs: valuation schedule per method, closing stock value per method, COGS per method, comparative delta strip

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** This is the missing "Class 3 stocks" complement to the Class 2 (Amortization, Depreciation) and Class 5 (Bank Reconciliation) playgrounds already present.

### 2.4 OHADA Payroll — Calcul de la fiche de paie (CNPS + IRPP)

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master / Practicing professional

**Pedagogical objective:** OHADA-zone payroll requires applying country-specific social contribution rates (CNPS, CAFAL/FDFP) and progressive IRPP brackets. Most students cannot derive net salary from gross, leading to systematic errors in the payroll journal entry and social-charge declarations.

**Core interaction:** The learner inputs gross salary, country (Cameroon / Côte d'Ivoire / Sénégal), contract type (CDI/CDD/CDDI), and optional transport/meal allowances. The playground itemises each deduction (CNPS employee, CNPS employer, IRPP, CAFAL), shows the net salary, and generates the SYSCOHADA payroll journal entry (accounts 661, 4211, 4311, 4321, 4341, etc.).

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: gross salary, country, contract type, non-taxable allowances
- Outputs: itemised payslip, employer vs employee cost breakdown, SYSCOHADA journal entry for the payroll charge and the settlement

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Payroll is the highest-frequency periodic journal entry in practice. It extends the Journal Entry playground into a domain-specific workflow and adds country-by-country OHADA compliance data (tax brackets, CNPS ceilings) not covered anywhere else.

### 2.5 Lease Accounting Comparison — Contrats de location : SYSCOHADA vs IFRS 16

**Target learner:** Master / Practicing professional (Big 4, multinational entities)

**Pedagogical objective:** SYSCOHADA's lease classification (operating vs finance) diverges materially from IFRS 16's right-of-use model, which requires all leases ≥ 12 months on the balance sheet. Professionals in dual-reporting environments must reconcile the two treatments; this gap is a frequent source of errors in first-time IFRS adoption projects.

**Core interaction:** The learner inputs lease terms (payment amount, frequency, duration, implicit rate, asset fair value). The playground classifies the lease under SYSCOHADA (operating if < 75% of economic life and PV of payments < 90% of FV) and generates the IFRS 16 amortisation table (ROU asset + liability). Side-by-side panels show annual P&L and balance-sheet impact under each standard, and the reconciliation delta.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: lease payment, frequency, total duration, implicit interest rate, asset fair value and economic life
- Outputs: SYSCOHADA classification + journal entry (operating: simple expense; finance: capitalised), IFRS 16 ROU asset schedule, lease liability schedule, annual delta reconciliation

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** The Amortization playground handles asset depreciation. This playground handles the lease-liability side and adds the comparative IFRS angle that is absent from all existing modules, meeting the needs of Master-level learners and professionals managing dual-standard reporting.
