### [HIGH] Missing CSS token `--orange` — hardcoded fallback always fires

**Files:**
- `src/lib/components/playground/KpiStrip.svelte:76`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/LedgerPanel.svelte:175`
- `src/lib/playgrounds/bank-reconciliation/components/VarianceScale.svelte:230`

`color: var(--orange, #f59e0b)` and `background: color-mix(in srgb, var(--orange, #f59e0b) …)` appear in multiple playground components. The token `--orange` is **never emitted** by `css-generator.ts` — the theme defines `--amber` (`#f5a623`), not `--orange`. Every instance silently falls through to the hardcoded hex, defeating the token system and making these colours invisible to future theme changes.

**Suggested fix:** Replace `var(--orange, …)` with `var(--amber)` across all call sites, or add `--orange: ${c.amber};` to `css-generator.ts` if a semantic distinction is intended.

### [MEDIUM] Hardcoded `#fff` in `EntryHistory.svelte`

**File:** `src/lib/playgrounds/journal-entry/components/EntryHistory.svelte:400`

```css
.btn-confirm {
  background: var(--error);
  border: 1px solid var(--error);
  color: #fff;          /* ← hardcoded */
}
```

`#fff` bypasses the token system. If the background theme ever changes (e.g., a light-mode variant), the button text will be invisible.

**Suggested fix:** Replace with `color: var(--bg);` — `var(--bg)` is the darkest surface colour in the midnight theme and provides the same visual contrast, while remaining theme-aware.

### Idea 1 — Inventory Valuation Methods

**EN:** Inventory Valuation (FIFO · CUMP · LIFO)  
**FR:** Évaluation des stocks (PEPS · CUMP · DEPS)

**Target learner:** Licence 2, early Licence 3

**Pedagogical objective:** Students consistently confuse FIFO (PEPS), LIFO (DEPS), and weighted average cost (CUMP — Coût Unitaire Moyen Pondéré). SYSCOHADA mandates CUMP; IFRS prohibits LIFO; US-GAAP allows all three. The gap between knowing the rule and understanding *why* the methods produce different results is where exam errors cluster.

**Core interaction:** The learner enters stock movements (initial stock, purchase lots with date/quantity/unit price, sales orders with date and quantity). The playground computes ending inventory value and COGS under each of the three methods simultaneously, then overlays the P&L and balance-sheet impact side-by-side.

**Minimum viable inputs:** opening stock (qty + unit cost), up to 10 purchase lots, up to 10 sales orders (all within one fiscal period).  
**Outputs:** COGS, ending inventory, gross margin, and a reconciliation table per method; optional journal entries for the closing inventory adjustment.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** The journal-entry playground already models the entry structure; this playground fills the upstream gap of *computing* the amounts that would flow into those entries. The bank-reconciliation playground addresses reconciliation; this addresses the parallel skill of stock-count reconciliation.

### Idea 2 — OHADA Payroll & Social Charges

**EN:** Payroll & Social Charges Calculator  
**FR:** Calculateur de salaire et de charges sociales OHADA

**Target learner:** Licence 3, practicing professional

**Pedagogical objective:** Social charge structures vary significantly across OHADA member states (CNPS in Cameroon and Côte d'Ivoire, IPRES/CSS in Sénégal). Students can recite the formula but almost always confuse gross salary, taxable base, net salary, total employer cost, and the deduction/contribution split. The payroll journal entry is one of the most complex entries students encounter in financial accounting.

**Core interaction:** Enter gross salary, select country/jurisdiction, employment type, and optional seniority. The playground instantly renders a payroll slip: employer social charges, employee deductions, income tax withholding (simplified IRPP bracket), net salary, and total employer cost. A second panel shows the full journal entry (Débit: comptes 661/664/665; Crédit: comptes 421/431/432/447).

**Minimum viable inputs:** Gross monthly salary, country (Cameroon / Côte d'Ivoire / Sénégal), seniority band.  
**Outputs:** Payroll slip breakdown, total employer cost vs. net salary ratio, full double-entry journal.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** Payroll costs flow into the income statement (Class 6) and are recognised via journal entries. This playground extends the journal-entry skill from simple trade transactions to the most frequently recurring complex entry in practice.

### Idea 3 — Financial Ratios Dashboard

**EN:** Financial Ratios Dashboard  
**FR:** Tableau de bord des ratios financiers

**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master

**Pedagogical objective:** Students compute ratios in isolation during exams but rarely develop an understanding of how ratios interact. Increasing a credit line improves cash but worsens the debt ratio; selling inventory for cash improves the quick ratio but changes the asset turnover. This playground builds systems thinking around a live balance sheet.

**Core interaction:** The learner enters a simplified balance sheet and income statement (or accepts a pre-populated SYSCOHADA-format sample enterprise). The dashboard displays 15+ ratios grouped into four categories (liquidity, solvency, profitability, activity) with traffic-light threshold indicators. Editing any line item causes all ratios to recompute in real time. A "what-if" panel allows toggling one structural change (e.g., "take on a 10 MXAF loan") and seeing the cascade.

**Minimum viable inputs:** Balance sheet line items aligned to SYSCOHADA classes 2–5 (actifs) and 1/4 (passifs + capitaux propres); income statement revenue/expense lines.  
**Outputs:** Solvability ratio, current ratio, quick ratio, debt-to-equity, gross margin, net margin, ROA, ROE, asset turnover, receivables days, payables days, inventory days — each with a benchmark band and plain-language commentary.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** The journal-entry playground teaches individual transactions; this teaches the aggregate view. Learners who complete both can observe how repeated journal entries shift the ratios — connecting micro (transaction) to macro (financial position).

### Idea 4 — Deferred Tax & Temporary Differences

**EN:** Deferred Tax Calculator (IAS 12 / SYSCOHADA)  
**FR:** Impôts différés et différences temporaires

**Target learner:** Master, practicing professional

**Pedagogical objective:** Deferred tax is the highest-failure topic in advanced SYSCOHADA and IFRS courses. The concept of "temporary difference" is abstract until a learner can see the carrying amount and tax base of the same asset diverge over time and watch the deferred tax liability shrink to zero at the end of the asset's life. Almost every candidate who fails this topic fails because they cannot visualise the convergence.

**Core interaction:** Enter an asset (cost, accounting depreciation method/life, tax depreciation method/life, corporate tax rate). The playground renders a period-by-period worksheet: carrying amount, tax base, temporary difference, deferred tax liability/asset, and the change to record each year. A chart overlays the two depreciation curves against the converging deferred tax balance. A journal entry panel shows the year-end deferred tax entry.

**Minimum viable inputs:** Asset cost, accounting method (straight-line, declining-balance), accounting useful life, tax method, tax useful life, tax rate.  
**Outputs:** Year-by-year deferred tax worksheet; cumulative chart; annual journal entries (Débit: 69/Crédit: 477 for a deferred tax liability, or the reverse for an asset).

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** The depreciation playground covers *calculating* depreciation; this extends it into the *tax* dimension and generates the accounting entries. It also reinforces the journal-entry playground's double-entry logic with one of the most conceptually demanding entries in the OHADA curriculum.

### Idea 5 — Foreign Currency Transactions

**EN:** Foreign Currency Transactions (IAS 21 / SYSCOHADA)  
**FR:** Transactions en devises étrangères (IAS 21 / SYSCOHADA)

**Target learner:** Licence 3, Master

**Pedagogical objective:** OHADA-zone enterprises transact heavily in EUR, USD, GBP, and regional currencies (XOF, XAF, which share a fixed peg). Students reliably make errors on three distinct moments: initial recognition (at the transaction rate), year-end revaluation (at the closing rate), and settlement (at the payment rate), especially on which side the exchange difference lands and in which account it is recorded.

**Core interaction:** The learner selects a transaction type (export receivable or import payable), enters the foreign currency amount, and supplies three rates: transaction date, year-end, settlement date. The playground generates three timestamped journal entries — initial recognition, year-end revaluation, and final settlement — and computes the net foreign exchange gain or loss across the full lifecycle, showing it clearly as the difference between the rates.

**Minimum viable inputs:** Transaction type (receivable / payable), foreign currency, amount, invoice date + rate, year-end rate, settlement date + rate.  
**Outputs:** Three journal entries (accounts 411/401 vs. 476/477 for unrealised differences; 776/676 for realised gains/losses); net forex result; sensitivity analysis showing how a ±5% rate movement affects the final gain/loss.

**Why it complements existing playgrounds:** The journal-entry playground already covers standard trade entries; this extends to the currency-adjustment layer that is consistently the exam differentiator at Licence 3 and Master level. The TVM playground provides context on why exchange rate expectations matter for forward-rate pricing.
