**[C-1] Server-boundary violation — client component imports `$lib/server/`**
- **File:** `src/lib/components/feedback/FeedbackForm.svelte:7`
- `import type { FeedbackType, FeedbackSeverity } from '$lib/server/db/types'`
- SvelteKit's Vite plugin enforces the `$lib/server` boundary by scanning import paths at build time, including `import type`. This will throw a build error and may surface server module internals to the client bundle.
- **Fix:** Move `FeedbackType`, `FeedbackSeverity`, and `FeedbackEntry` out of `$lib/server/db/types.ts` into a shared file such as `src/lib/types/feedback.ts`. Both `FeedbackForm.svelte` and `validation.ts` import only these type-aliases — none require server-only runtime code.

### Idea 1 — SYSCOHADA Balance Sheet Builder
**EN:** SYSCOHADA Balance Sheet Builder · **FR:** Constructeur de Bilan SYSCOHADA

**Target learner:** Licence 2 / Licence 3 in accounting or finance

**Pedagogical objective:** Students struggle to map individual accounts to the correct SYSCOHADA balance sheet presentation (classes 1–5 on the liabilities side, classes 2–5 on the assets side, contra-accounts). The common misconception is treating the SYSCOHADA bilan as a simple left/right split identical to IFRS.

**Core interaction:** The learner is given a randomised trial balance (10–20 accounts, mix of asset/liability/equity/income summary). They drag each account line to the correct section of the SYSCOHADA bilan format (Actif Immobilisé, Actif Circulant, Capitaux Propres, Dettes Financières, Passif Circulant, Trésorerie-Passif). Amounts update in real time; reclassification errors are highlighted with the correct target section.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Input: pre-populated trial balance rows (account number, name, debit/credit balance)
- Output: completed bilan with totals, pass/fail grading per section, score card

**Complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry covers individual transactions; this playground covers the aggregation step — from posted ledger balances to a published financial statement. Bridges the two.

### Idea 2 — Inventory Valuation (CUMP / FIFO)
**EN:** Inventory Valuation Methods · **FR:** Méthodes de Valorisation des Stocks

**Target learner:** Licence 2 / Licence 3

**Pedagogical objective:** SYSCOHADA mandates CUMP (coût unitaire moyen pondéré) as the primary inventory valuation method, but students confuse CUMP-periodic vs. CUMP-perpetual, and do not understand how FIFO produces different gross margins under inflation. This gap causes errors in the cost of goods sold calculation and stock account balances.

**Core interaction:** The learner inputs a sequence of purchase lots (date, qty, unit cost) and sales events (date, qty). The playground computes running inventory under CUMP-periodic, CUMP-perpetual, and FIFO in parallel columns. Selecting different methods animates the stock card rows; the gross margin difference ribbon highlights how method choice affects net income.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: up to 10 purchase lines, up to 8 sales lines, opening stock
- Outputs: stock card table per method, closing stock value, COGS, gross margin comparison, journal entries for COGS recognition

**Complements existing playgrounds:** Depreciation covers fixed-asset allocation over time; this covers current-asset cost-flow — the managerial-accounting mirror image of the same concept.

### Idea 3 — Cash Flow Statement Builder (Indirect Method)
**EN:** Cash Flow Statement — Indirect Method · **FR:** Tableau des Flux de Trésorerie (Méthode Indirecte)

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master 1

**Pedagogical objective:** Students who can produce a correct P&L and balance sheet often fail to reconcile net income to operating cash flow. The indirect method's non-cash add-backs (depreciation, provisions, working-capital movements) are systematically misclassified. SYSCOHADA's tableau des flux de trésorerie follows IAS 7 structure, which differs from the French PCG layout they may have seen in prior courses.

**Core interaction:** The learner is given a simplified income statement and two-year comparative balance sheet (pre-filled). They classify each balance sheet movement (ΔDebtors, ΔInventory, ΔCreditors, depreciation charge, gain on disposal, etc.) as operating/investing/financing and choose the sign. A running cash total updates live; the three-section tally reconciles to the closing cash balance shown on the balance sheet.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: net income, depreciation charge, 5–8 working-capital line movements, investing and financing items
- Outputs: completed IAS-7-style cash flow statement, operating/investing/financing subtotals, error indicators per line

**Complements existing playgrounds:** Bank Reconciliation verifies the cash *ledger* balance; this playground explains *how* that cash balance was generated from operations — the conceptual layer above reconciliation.

### Idea 4 — VAT / TVA Calculator (OHADA Zone)
**EN:** VAT Computation & Accounting · **FR:** Calcul et Comptabilisation de la TVA

**Target learner:** Licence 2 / practicing professional

**Pedagogical objective:** TVA in OHADA member states has country-specific rates (Cameroon 19.25%, Ivory Coast 18%, Senegal 18%, etc.) and the distinction between TVA collectée / TVA déductible / TVA à décaisser is a frequent exam failure point. Students apply the wrong rate or mispost the TVA balance account (445x class) entirely.

**Core interaction:** The learner selects an OHADA country (rate auto-fills), enters HT (pre-tax) purchase and sale amounts, and must: (1) compute TVA collectée and TVA déductible, (2) determine the net TVA position, (3) record the journal entries for both purchase and sale. The playground validates each field, highlights the 445x accounts used, and shows a mini T-account to confirm the balance carried forward.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: country selector, 3–5 purchase lines (HT), 3–5 sale lines (HT)
- Outputs: TVA computation grid, net TVA to pay / credit, journal entries with correct 445x accounts, tax return summary

**Complements existing playgrounds:** Journal Entry teaches the mechanics; this playground provides a domain-specific scenario that appears in every SYSCOHADA exam and professional tax return.

### Idea 5 — Provisions & Impairment (SYSCOHADA Rules)
**EN:** Provisions & Asset Impairment · **FR:** Provisions et Dépréciations d'Actifs

**Target learner:** Licence 3 / Master 1

**Pedagogical objective:** Students conflate *provisions pour risques* (liabilities) with *dépréciations* (contra-asset reductions) and misuse account classes 39x, 49x, 59x vs. 15x. The SYSCOHADA rules on when to recognise, reverse, and disclose provisions differ subtly from IFRS IAS 37, causing errors in professional contexts where both standards must be understood.

**Core interaction:** The learner is presented with 6–8 scenario cards (bad debt, lawsuit pending, inventory obsolescence, investment decline). They must classify each as *provision pour risques et charges* or *dépréciation*, select the correct SYSCOHADA account group, enter the amount, and record the journal entry (expense vs. contra-asset debit). Subsequent-period scenarios test reversal entries.

**Minimum viable inputs / outputs:**
- Inputs: scenario selection, classification choice, account picker from SYSCOHADA chart, amount
- Outputs: journal entry validation, account-group feedback, score per scenario, summary of all provision/depreciation entries for the period

**Complements existing playgrounds:** Depreciation covers planned straight-line/reducing-balance write-down; this playground covers unplanned impairment and contingent liabilities — the judgment-heavy counterpart.
